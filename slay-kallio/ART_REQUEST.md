# Slay Kallio — Art Request

## Core visual idea

Slay Kallio should use the existing TURF character concepts as the identity base, but reinterpret them as **physical standing cardboard cut-outs in a side-view paper-theatre world**.

Think of the readability and charm of a flat paper-character game such as Paper Mario, but with a rougher Kallio street-art / handmade-diorama treatment rather than copying Nintendo designs. Characters should look like illustrated figures printed or painted onto card, cut to silhouette, then stood upright in a shallow 3D scene.

The important shift is: **TURF supplies the people; Slay Kallio supplies the material language.**

## Character construction

Each character is a flat standee:

- front face carries the detailed TURF-derived illustration
- silhouette is clean and immediately readable from the side
- visible card thickness on turns, flips, falls and extreme poses
- slightly rough cut edge rather than perfectly sterile vector edges
- optional exposed corrugation / layered card edge on selected close shots
- feet meet a simple cardboard tab, foot, slot or hidden standing base
- no realistic 3D body volume; depth comes from the physical card itself
- clothing, hair and weapons may be separate overlapping card pieces where animation benefits

The result should feel like someone made a very good tabletop game / theatre set from printed cardboard versions of Kallio street characters.

## Camera and world

This is **not TURF isometric**. Slay Kallio is primarily horizontal side-view.

- characters read left/right
- level geometry can have shallow 2.5D depth
- foreground and background can be layered paper/cardboard scenery
- characters remain visibly flat even when the environment has depth
- use parallax, folds, slots, taped seams and layered boards to sell a physical miniature set

## Animation language

Do not redraw characters as fully volumetric actors. Animate the standees as physical paper objects.

Useful techniques:

- whole-card translation and tilt
- squash through slight card bend / hinge rather than flesh deformation
- separate limb card pieces for strong attacks or runs
- head, arm, weapon or coat pieces pivoting on hidden paper fasteners
- fast left/right flip can briefly expose the thin cardboard edge
- jumps can cant the whole standee forward/back
- impacts can make the standee wobble on its base
- KO can become a literal fall-flat card motion
- extreme hits may spin the card briefly edge-on before it lands

A small amount of deliberately theatrical motion is preferable to trying to fake conventional skeletal 3D animation.

## TURF concept reuse

Use the supplied TURF concept pool for:

- faces and character identity
- clothing
- weapons and props
- silhouette
- body type
- attitude
- color relationships

Do **not** inherit TURF's isometric-facing restrictions. Side/profile concepts that were failures for TURF are particularly valuable here.

Priority source types in the v0.2 pack:

1. explicit side-walk and profile sheets
2. four-direction character turnarounds
3. TURF near-profile attack frames rejected from isometric production
4. broad TURF roster sheets for identity mining
5. clothing / prop breakdown sheets

## First prototype character

Start with one strong silhouette from the pack and make a minimal physical-cardboard set:

1. neutral side idle
2. 4–6 frame walk/run
3. simple attack
4. hit wobble
5. KO / fall-flat
6. left/right coverage by a clean flip or mirrored authored art where asymmetry requires it

The first test should prove the **cardboard object illusion**, not animation volume.

## Material target

Characters should read as:

- printed ink / paint on matte card
- slightly fibrous paper texture
- shallow worn edges
- occasional creases, scuffs, pen marks or print misregistration
- subtle real-world shadow cast onto scenery, but no baked sprite shadow
- grounded by physical contact with the miniature set

Avoid glossy plastic, clay, plush, toy-figurine or full 3D-character rendering.

## Environment relationship

The world can mix:

- cardboard architecture
- paper posters
- folded street signs
- layered asphalt / pavement sheets
- corrugated walls
- taped repairs
- hand-cut fences
- miniature practical lights

Kallio should still be recognizable through architecture, signage, colors and street details, but everything is filtered through the handmade stage-set language.

## Production rule

Preserve two things above all else:

1. **TURF character identity**
2. **the unmistakable flat-cardboard physicality**

If a render starts looking like a normal 3D person, it has moved away from the target.
