# Art sheet request: WORLD 6 — RIVER MODULES

For Grok (owner, 2026-10-31: "you can build all levels/worlds now"). Same
format as the World 3 and 4 requests and the approved World 2 sheet. Claude
built World 6 from this brief in v72; a sheet the owner approves can still
change it.

---

Make ONE environment art sheet in exactly the style and layout of the approved
"World 2 — Curb / Lawn Modules" sheet: white background, watercolour with clean
ink lines, 8 numbered modules with hand-lettered labels, each with its size in
metres. Title: "Toko Drop 2.0 environment art sheet / WORLD 6 — RIVER
MODULES". Mark it PENDING APPROVAL.

The world: out of the pipes into a river at dusk. The arena is a stretch of
dark running water, 68 × 24 m, pebbled at both banks, seen from above at about
30°; the river runs on past both ends. Salmon blobs (SALMA) live here, and the
boss is PIKE. The floor leans with a current that turns; lily pads drift the
way it pushes.

Rules for every module:
- Micro Machines scale: the 1 m hero is small next to them. Draw the hero
  beside module 1 for scale.
- Simple solid shapes a 3D artist can build from boxes, cylinders and lathes.
- The floor stays mid-dark and quiet, so the bright jelly enemies read on it.
- Nothing stands along the near side.
- No characters, no letters, no creatures, no spectators.

The 8 modules:
1. River stone: a rounded boulder, 0.9–2.5 m, moss on its top, dark and wet
   at the waterline.
2. Reeds: a clump of tall thin blades, 1.1–2 m, with two or three cattails.
3. Lily pad: a round leaf with a notch cut to its middle, 0.6–0.9 m, a few
   with a pale pink flower.
4. Pile: an old wooden post standing in the water, 0.6–1.6 m above it, the
   water foaming round its foot.
5. Washed-up log: 2.8–4.6 m, bark down its length, rings at its ends.
6. Riverbank: wet mud at the water, then grass.
7. Running water: the river's dark water with streaks running down it, and
   the pebbled bank at its edge.
8. Arena layout (guide only): the river from above at 30°, stones, reeds and
   logs on the far bank, rows of piles across the water past each end.
