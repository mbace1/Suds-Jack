# Piritori → Eden narrative baseline

Status: owner-directed canon for the first playable baseline, 2026-08-17.

## Premise

Two generations mistake movement for escape. In the 2000s, Aatami builds an
amphetamine business around Piritori while his artist brother Jaska refuses the
logic of the ledger. In 2024, Aatami’s son Kalle inherits the routes in Pasila
as Alpha-PVP displaces part of the older stimulant market. Kalle’s brother Aaro
inherits none of the appetite for it.

The game draws from *East of Eden* only at the level of themes: brothers,
inheritance, rivalry, the desire to choose differently and the ambiguity of
“Eden.” It does not adapt its scenes, prose or characters.

## Generations

### Aatami / Kallio / 2000s

- Aatami begins at Piritori with cash, debt, a small bag and one known route.
- Jaska is a stoner, artist and moral counterweight who hates the business but
  does not exist merely to deliver lectures.
- The fictional McCormick family controls bars and restaurants around the
  Siltanen/Kuudes Linja complex. They sell firearms, armour, hired guns,
  intimidation and sabotage. Their people must feel like a large family rather
  than a generic weapons shop.
- The fictional Jade Lantern Network is the in-world name for the Chinese
  restaurant-mafia power described in the pitch. It grows through restaurant
  fronts across Kallio and specialises in firearms and illicit materials. Its
  members must be written as individuals rather than an ethnic stereotype.
- Toko Slomo, a Japanese noodle chef on Vaasankatu, is Aatami’s friend and only
  imperfect source inside the group’s expansion. His shop sells information
  and lets Aatami risk money or favour on uncertain sabotage.
- Piritori starts as the cheapest small-quantity market and cheapest source of
  hired operatives. Aatami’s first firearm is bought there and unlocks armed
  missions. In the long run Aatami calls the shots while his crew performs the
  work, suffers wounds and may die.
- Aatami eventually retires to Pasila with his sons Kalle and Aaro. He calls the
  move Eden, but carries the network with him.

#### Era I material culture

- The first campaign is anchored in 2003: euro cash is current, while old
  markka notes and coins still turn up in drawers, jars and informal payments.
- Markka is not spendable. It becomes euro only after a physical bank-teller
  visit. The fixed conversion is not the challenge; travel time, opening hours,
  who carries the cash and the visibility of the visit are.
- Television is the authoritative public clock. Arvo's scheduled broadcasts
  introduce major events and make the city feel collectively informed.
- Mobile phones are for calls and SMS. They are tools for terse orders and
  relationship messages, never miniature computers or modern app menus.
- Going online requires reaching a desktop terminal at a home, office, library
  or internet cafe. Online information can therefore have a place, cost and
  delay.

### Kalle / Pasila / 2024

- Kalle takes over and pushes beyond Pasila.
- Aaro is a sensitive rock musician whose passion and value are independent of
  whether he can redeem Kalle.
- Police pressure radiates from Pasila. Closures and surveillance use the same
  map as commuters, shoppers and vulnerable people.
- Amphetamine remains present but expensive and uneven. Alpha-PVP is cheaper,
  volatile and mechanically profitable; every sale raises harm, distrust and
  enforcement pressure more sharply.
- In 2025, Aaro independently leaves for the war in Ukraine and dies. This is a
  fixed tragedy outside the economy: never a mission, optimisation, reward or
  consequence of the player selecting the “wrong” dialogue.

## The enforcement feedback loop

The story treats the following as a central political argument:

1. a 2023 international investigation seized a roughly 100-kilogram
   amphetamine shipment destined for Finland;
2. market scarcity and quality changes made room for a more damaging substitute;
3. Alpha-PVP’s visible harms increased calls, surveillance and operations;
4. the expanded crisis created arguments for more police powers, equipment and
   institutional resources.

Items 1 and the later substitution/harms are grounded in published sources.
Direct intent, a single-cause chain and “police profit” are not established by
those sources. In the drama, Kalle asserts the strongest version. The fictional
broadcaster Arvo Linde distinguishes documented event, inference and accusation.
The simulation expresses the systemic irony without claiming that a real officer
or institution deliberately created the Alpha-PVP market.

## Voice and visual narrative

- Arvo Linde is a fictional homage to Finnish newsreader Arvi Lind: he borrows
  Lind’s calm authority, precise delivery and formal late-analogue studio
  presence. He is not a literal portrait, and nothing Arvo reports should be
  read as an action or opinion of the real journalist.
- News cards are terse, dated and specific about source status.
- Era I screens treat TV, SMS and online access as three distinct channels with
  different rhythms. No smartphone visual language appears in the 2003 layer.
- People remain people. “Zombie” may appear only as a dehumanising word a
  character or headline uses and the narrative challenges.
- The map is a character: familiar routes repeat across generations while their
  meanings and pressures change.
- Humour is dry and relational; suffering is not scenery or slapstick.

## Fiction boundary

All named characters, families, restaurant groups, deals and routes are
fictional or fictionalised composites. Real news supplies public conditions and
mission patterns, never unproven accusations against named people. The game is
not a guide to trafficking, concealment, dosing, weapons or evading police.
