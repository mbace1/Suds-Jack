The logotype is drawn, not set. It only ever says three words, so those words are outlines in js/wordmark.js — no font file to licence, to load, or to fail to load. They are drawn after the owner's face, by eye: close, and ours. If the original vector artwork surfaces, its outlines belong in GLYPHS.

Who is signing this

Toko Midori is the masked artist behind the look of every cabinet in this workshop. The mask is not a gimmick — it is the terms. You do not get a face, a founder story or a personal brand to buy into. You get the work, and the work is free, in a browser, with no account.

Toko is not against the machine. Toko is holding one — these games are built with AI, out loud, on purpose, and nobody is pretending otherwise. What Toko is against is the machine becoming the audience: a world where everything is generated and nothing is made, where people scroll output instead of cutting their own.

Use the tools. Take the source. GO MAKE YOUR OWN.

The face

Four fat round-capped arcs and two stems. The mouth is two arcs opening up, nested. Each eye is one arc opening down with a stem dropped from the inside of its crown — that stem is what cuts the two slots that make an eye an eye. One stroke weight throughout. No fills, no corners, no straight lines except those two stems.

It is drawn from one geometry table in js/face.js. The canvas strokes it; the SVG exporter emits the same arcs as path data. The file you hand a printer and the thing on screen are the same object.

Two colours

Both are process primaries — 100% K and 100% M. That is the whole idea: it prints anywhere, on any press, at no cost, with nothing to match. White is the paper, not a colour. There are no tints, shades, gradients or drop shadows.

Magenta is 4.0:1 on black. It is a mark colour and a large-display colour — never body copy.

Lockups

Face, gap, three lines, ™. The logotype stands the same height as the face; that relationship is the lockup, and nothing else about it is adjustable.

The one, and the clusters

Toko Midori is the one, the person — the face reversed out of a bust. That is the character rather than the mark: what goes on a sign, a pin, a stage.

But also Toko Midori is clusters. The same head, packed with small heads — Toko, the members, the ex-members, the members who have not arrived yet, and the players. One person and a crowd, drawn with the same stamp. The crowd is what the person is made of, so it never wins the silhouette: the big face still reads on top.

The carriers

The face also runs in a rotating set of flats — the mark in a colour on white, and reversed out of a full-bleed colour tile. One face, many carriers. This does not make them brand colours: black and magenta are the identity, and these are what the mark is carried on, one flat at a time, never two inside a single mark.

Both ways round, at icon size — the tile and the mark on paper.

Several of these pairings — white on yellow especially — are nowhere near a text contrast ratio. They carry the mark and never type.

The counter

A slim bar that sits at the top of the arcade and opens into a conversation with Toko in the old Sierra idiom — a portrait, text that types itself out, and a numbered list of things you are allowed to say. Police Quest at the front desk.

It is a hand-written dialogue tree, not a language model. No network call, ever: the kit is offline-first and zero-dependency, and a workshop whose whole position is go make your own should not answer you with rented autocomplete. Toko says what Toko wrote — in js/dialogue.js, which is the file to edit.

It runs both ways. You can type at him on the > line — word overlap against a lookup, and a miss says so rather than answering the wrong question. You can leave him a note, which goes out through the arcade's own transport and is never reported as delivered when it was not. He can ask you something, hand you a sticker he draws on the spot, read back the scores the cabinets left on your machine, and talk about one cabinet at a time.

1–9 pick a topic · type to talk · ESC leaves. Asking some things unlocks others. On this page there is no catalogue behind it, so the cabinet answers say so rather than throwing.

The glitch

Seeded, so the same hit lands the same way twice — a glitch you cannot reproduce is a bug wearing a costume. And an event, not a state: below about 0.25 the mark reads clean, because a logo that is permanently broken reads as a rendering fault and nobody trusts a rendering fault.

The sting

    
The face draws itself — the mouth sweeps open, the eyes drop in, it
    blinks, the logotype lands. About three seconds, on magenta. Skippable on
    any input from the very first frame: a sting you cannot skip is an ad, and
    this workshop does not run ads.
