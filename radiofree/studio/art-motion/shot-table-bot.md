## Sources

The toy in the study is Gabbo, by Curio Interactive; ours is unbranded. The study is named because the story is unclear without it (README, "Naming in the films").

- University of Cambridge, Faculty of Education, AI in the Early Years project — report and news story, March 2026 ("ai toys study play", cam.ac.uk/stories/ai-toys-study-play; content.educ.cam.ac.uk/node/8976): children up to five observed playing with Gabbo; when a three-year-old told it "I'm sad", it misheard and replied: "Don't worry! I'm a happy little bot. Let's keep the fun going. What shall we talk about next?"; researchers said this may have signalled the child's sadness was unimportant. Coverage: Talker (syndicated, e.g. chicotnewspapers.com), ghll.org.uk (13.3.2026), OECD AI incident 2026-03-11.
- In pretend play a child offered the toy an imaginary present and it refused and moved on (same coverage).
- Recommendations: manufacturers should test products with children and consult safeguarding specialists before release; keep such toys in shared family spaces. Funded by the KPMG Foundation and Ethos Foundation.
