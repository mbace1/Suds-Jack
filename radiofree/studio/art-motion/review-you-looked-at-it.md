# Review: 成片.mp4 (15.0 s, 1080x1920, H.264 30 fps + AAC 48 kHz stereo)

Method: 60 frames at 4 fps on 5 contact sheets plus full-size crops at each cut. Frame-difference motion measured at 10 fps. Audio checked with ebur128, silencedetect and 0.1 s / 10 ms RMS and peak windows. Hard cuts detected at **3.2, 6.5, 9.7 and 12.9 s**, which gives five shots of about 3 s each.

## Verdict: SHIP WITH FIXES — 6 / 10

The style holds together. Aged paper, red and black, halftone, condensed block type, rain streaks and birch leaves run through every shot. Nothing reads as generic AI slop: no blue-purple gradients and no bean-people. The receipt gag in shot 3 is the best thing in the film and lands. The weak points are the opening and the middle. Shot 1 does not say "Helsinki" or "glasses". Shot 4 is three seconds of an almost empty frame. A hand carries a smile line, which is a face on an object. The audio has a transient chopped off on the last frame and a dead hole under the key action. The real-event facts (the wake word being spoken, $249) barely come through.

## Problems

### RED — must fix before delivery

1. **14.90–15.00: audio is chopped on the final frame.** A new hit starts at 14.90 s with a peak of about −6 dBFS. It is cut off 100 ms later at the end of the file (15.00 s: −51 dB, then nothing). On a phone this sounds like a glitch or a broken export, and it is the last thing heard.
   *Fix:* move the final hit to about 14.2 s so it lands with the pocket ping at 14.25, and let it ring out under the hold. Alternatively, end the music on a tail with a 150–250 ms fade, or add a 0.5 s visual hold and extend the file.

2. **9.7–12.9: shot 4 (the LOOK = BUY billboard) is close to dead air.** For about 3 s the frame is a billboard on a frame structure with the lower 60% of the screen empty. There are no people, no hand, no Helsinki and no action, only a slow pull-back. On a phone the eye has nothing to do after the first second. Between 9.0 and 9.5 the billboard also enters with the top of "LOOK" cut off by the frame edge.
   *Fix:* put the action in the empty lower half. One option is a row of figures from behind at a tram stop, all glasses-wearers, each with a red glint while the billboard looks back. Another is to cut the shot to about 1.2 s and give the time to shots 1 and 5. Start the shot with "LOOK" fully inside the frame.

3. **6.5–9.5 and 12.2–13.0: the hands have a smile drawn on them.** The black fist holding the receipt, and the fist dropping the glasses, both carry a curved light line across the knuckles that reads as a smiling mouth. The fist then looks like a cartoon character. That breaks the "no faces" rule and is the one place the film slips into cute-object territory.
   *Fix:* remove the curve, or redraw it as straight knuckle creases. Give the hands a wrist or cuff, ideally a coat sleeve, so they belong to a person rather than floating as mascots.

### YELLOW — should fix

4. **0.0–3.2: the opening does not read as Helsinki or as glasses within a second.**
   - The landmark silhouette (bulbous central dome flanked by two towers) reads as the Taj Mahal or a mosque, not Helsinki Cathedral. Helsinki Cathedral has a green central dome, four small cupolas and a columned portico.
   - The black half-disc umbrella sits behind the figure's head with no arm holding it, so it reads as a fan or a setting sun as much as an umbrella.
   - The glasses are tiny gold stubs at the ears. The red glint arrives at about 2.25 s, very late in a 3.2 s shot.

   *Fix:* draw the cathedral correctly (four cupolas, portico, steps) or use the Senate Square steps. Put a hand and handle on the umbrella. Make the glasses' temples visible and fire the glint by about 1.0 s.

5. **Throughout: the wake-word mechanic and the price never appear.** "SAY ITS NAME" is typed on, but nobody says the name: no speech mark, no word balloon, no wake-word pulse. The $249 price is absent, so a viewer gets "glasses buy things" but not "you summon it by name, and it costs $249".
   *Fix:* at about 1.5 s, add a red Constructivist speech wedge from the figure with a nonsense name in block letters. Add "$249" to the receipt total (for example "TOTAL: $249 + EVERYTHING") or to the billboard.

6. **3.2–6.4: shot 2 sits static for long stretches, and the right lens is unreadable.** Apart from falling leaves, the frame barely changes from 3.5 to 4.2 s and from 4.7 to 6.4 s (motion near the floor of the leaf-only level). The left lens (cake) reads. The right lens shows a birch trunk with a diagonal grey smear that reads as a stain, not a branch or a leaf. The bottom half of the frame stays empty until "ORDERED" lands at 4.3 s.
   *Fix:* turn the smear into one clear birch leaf in the lens, and stamp a second word under it ("ALSO ORDERED?") at about 5.3 s. Alternatively, trim the shot to about 2.2 s.

7. **12.8–13.95: about 1.1 s of near-silence (−25 to −35 dB) under the key action.** This is the hand dropping the glasses into the coat pocket. The silence works as a pause, but the drop itself has no sound.
   *Fix:* keep the quiet but add a soft fabric thump at about 13.7 s when the glasses enter the pocket, so the 14.05 s hit and the ping read as a response.

8. **Throughout: "early dark" is not felt.** Every shot is bright cream paper. The rain streaks and leaves come through. The Helsinki dark at 4 pm does not.
   *Fix:* darken the paper toward ochre-grey, and push shot 4 and shot 5's upper paper toward dusk with a lit window or a street lamp. Constructivist posters regularly used black fields.

9. **7.75–9.5: the receipt scrolls its header off the top of the frame.** "GLANCE · RECEIPT" and the first line are cut by the frame top while new lines are still landing. Six items in about 3 s is also dense.
   *Fix:* hold the header in place and let the paper extend downward, or shrink the type slightly. Consider dropping one item (VET VISIT or PIGEON) to give each stamp about 0.6 s.

10. **0.75–3.2: "NAME" overlaps the top edge of the photo panel**, and the red sun is clipped behind the panel. The overlap is plausibly a deliberate Lissitzky move, but at phone size the lower edge of NAME merges with the dark halftone.
    *Fix:* either commit to the overlap (put a paper-coloured knockout under NAME) or lift NAME about 20 px clear of the panel.

### GREEN — nitpicks

11. **Loudness:** −13.0 LUFS integrated, true peak −2.1 dBFS. That is fine for social, but slightly hotter than the −14 LUFS most platforms normalise to, so it will be turned down. A true-peak ceiling of −1 dBTP is fine either way.
12. **Cuts:** all four are hard cuts with no Constructivist wipe or diagonal slash. A 2–3 frame red diagonal wipe on at least one cut would push the style further.
13. **6.5 s cut:** there is no distinct audio accent on this cut, while 3.2 s and 9.6–9.7 s both get one. Re-time a hit onto it.
14. **"=BUY" crosses the foot of "LOOK" on the billboard.** This is in keeping with the style, but at phone size the "K" loses its lower leg.

## What works
- **One consistent style** from first frame to last: aged paper, red and black, halftone photo-panels, condensed sans block letters, diagonal red and black bars. Nothing off-style intrudes.
- **The receipt gag (6.5–9.5)** is the joke and it escalates properly: CAKE ORDERED → VET VISIT BOOKED → FLIGHT CHECKING → BIRCH LEAF ? → PIGEON FOLLOWED → SKY PENDING. The red stamps land on audio onsets about every 0.5 s.
- **Audio–picture sync is generally good.** There are hits at the 3.2 s cut and on "ORDERED" (about 4.3 s), the stamps follow the beat, and the 2.4–3.1 s breath before the reveal works.
- **The ending lands on an action, not a title card.** The glasses go into the pocket, the pocket pings red, and the type reads "STILL LOOKING". That is a good closing punchline.
- **Drizzle and birch leaves** are present and alive in every shot, so the picture never fully freezes.
- **No faces on people.** People appear only from behind and as hands, apart from the hand-smile issue above.
