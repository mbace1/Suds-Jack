# Same Trick, Two Verdicts: pitch, 18 Oct (replaces The Scoreboard)

Papercut, ~32 s + slate. An EU/US story, so a 12:50 release. Research: `research-satire-history.md` §10 (Berlin) and
§12 (California), both well documented; EU AI Act Article 50(4) confirmed in the regulation's text and in current
guidance.

**Why this replaces The Scoreboard:** the owner said the Scoreboard gave "only the headlines (King won.. ok)". This
one shows the trick itself, why each court decided as it did, and what the rule is now. It is two cases, not five.

**The one question:** Can a joke put words in a leader's mouth with AI? **Two courts, two answers.**

**As built (18 Oct, key `tv`):** seen from above on a satirist's green cutting mat, not The Scoreboard's room. The
trick is a paper puppet: a blank head (nobody's face) whose jaw is a separate cut-out piece, dropped open by a strip
of cyan AI voice. The court's answers arrive from above, as things do on a table: a stamp, a sticker, a gavel. The
puppets talk in a wordless toy-speaker babble synced to the jaw, so nobody is quoted. Caption 2 was cut to three lines.

**What the viewer leaves with:** the same trick, and the difference is a label. Europe now asks for one, kept small
for satire; California's court refused to require one.

**Rules and risks:**
- **Berlin:** a preliminary injunction (Landgericht Berlin II, 15 O 579/23, reported 25 Feb 2024). "Not clearly
  recognisable as fake or satire" is a paraphrase (FAZ via turi2), so it is never in quote marks. No appeal outcome was
  found, so the caption says "banned", not "finally".
- **California:** AB 2839 was blocked on 2 Oct 2024 and struck down in August 2025. "Answer a joke with more speech" is
  our plain-language summary of the order's counter-speech reasoning, so it is not quoted.
- **Article 50(4)** applies from 2 Aug 2026. For evidently satirical work, disclosure is limited to saying the
  content exists, "in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work".
- **Naming:** roles, not names: "the German chancellor", "a candidate". The film shows the rulings, not the parodies'
  lines.
