## Rules and risks

- **No candidate, party or party colour.** The candidates are masked Tokos at blank podiums. The partisan split is
  left out of the picture and the captions. **No cash**: money is only the counter's digits.
- **Nothing implies lawbreaking.** Unlabelled is not illegal in most places (no federal rule, and state laws differ
  in scope). The film says what the ads *said*, not that anyone broke a rule. The aide is a neutral figure, not a
  villain.
- **"AI-made" includes enhanced** ("at least partly with AI"). A viewer may still read the ten sets as deepfakes. The
  copy never says "fake".
- **"About 30 states"** is approximate. Wesleyan says 30; NCSL counts 28 with disclosure among 31 with laws; two
  laws were struck down; some are narrow (e.g. only deceptive depictions of candidates). "Some AI disclosure" carries
  that.
- **The exact labelled share differs by source** (27% / ~30% / 31%). The film says "about 3 in 10", and nothing more
  exact is on screen.
- **The primary release was not opened.** All figures come from search excerpts of two or more outlets. Open
  Wesleyan's 15 Oct release before air if the proxy allows.
- **"By mid-October"**: the data runs through 11 Oct and the release came out on 15 Oct. The post says "By 11 Oct".
- **Disclosure for the editor:** this film was researched and built by Claude, Anthropic's AI system, and it is a
  film about AI in political ads. Anthropic is itself active in US AI policy. The film names no AI tool or firm and
  takes no side on any bill. Please check the balance.
- fi and ja are drafts (`native_read` false). The fi "spent" caption needs 6.9 s against 5.6 s, fi "law" 5.7 s
  against 5.5 s, ja "question" 6.0 s against 5.6 s and ja "spent" 8.0 s against 5.6 s. Those masters need holds or
  shorter lines.
