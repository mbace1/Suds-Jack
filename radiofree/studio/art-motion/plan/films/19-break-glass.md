**The one question:** *When does a temporary law stop being temporary?*

**The answer, in one sentence (copy.json `argument`):** Finland's temporary border law of 2024 has never been used. It
was extended once, and now two more years are proposed, unchanged; a committee agreed. Never used, still temporary,
proposed until 2028.

## Palette: a winter night at the border, in card
Night sky in cut indigo bands (`#06080f` → `#33425f`), a cream moon; spruce in two depths (`#1c2c3c` far, `#14261f`
near, snow on the tips); snow `#dde4ea` with blue shade `#b5c1cd`. The guardhouse and the wall are weathered blue-grey
boards (`#3b4754`), so the **red case** (`#c0392b`, cream plate) is the loudest thing in every close shot. The tag is
the house ochre (`#e3a33a`); the first year is written in black ink, every later one in the red marker (`#d8402f`).
The desk is dark wood with a green blotter; the stamp's ink is a muted green (`#2f7a4f`). Warm lamp light (`#ffe2a0`)
is the one glow.

## The motion technique that carries the twist
**MARK** (rough.js 4.6.6, `scenes/vendor/rough-4.6.6.js`, as `ml_mark` in `scenes/ml_kit.js`): the marker's strike
through each year and the new year written in after it, left to right as if by hand: 2025 → 2026 on the wall, 2026 →
2028 on the desk, then the circle round 2028. Every mark is drawn along its own path length, so it is a pure function
of the shot's time. Behind it, the **time-lapse** of shot 3 (daylight bands, snow, ice, dust, a web) is what makes
"never been used" visible rather than told.

## Risks and how the film handles them
- **The people at the border are not the subject.** No people anywhere: no asylum seekers, no crowd, no guards. The
  border is a lowered barrier in the snow.
- **The threat is not argued.** Nothing says or shows whether the law is needed. The film asks only how "temporary"
  is decided: a law kept for years and never used.
- **Names.** The Act by its own English name (on the booklet), "Border Security Act" (the government's own short
  name) on the proposal, never "pushback law" or "käännytyslaki". The committee is unnamed in the caption and in the English
  corner tag (COMMITTEE STATEMENT, which fits beside the button rail), and named in the Finnish and Japanese tags; the
  Chancellor of Justice is not in the film.
- **Pending vote.** "Proposed" in the caption, PLENARY VOTE: STILL TO COME in the last tag; never "extended to 2028".
- **Party colours** stay off: the two DISSENT cards are cream and red ink, like everything else marked by hand.
- **"Not applied" vs "never used":** the government's own wording is the corner tag; the caption says the plain
  version.
