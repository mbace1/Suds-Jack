## Three devices

1. **The label (picked).** One generic jug, and every 2026 beat happens to its label:
   - a STATE CANCER WARNING sticker can't stick (Durnell);
   - a rubber stamp brands it NATIONAL DEFENSE PRIORITY (the order);
   - a NONNEGOTIABLE sticky note lands beside a desk phone that never rings (Means alleges);
   - the warning box stays empty.
2. **The rodeo round-up.** This was the owner's hint. A cowboy lassoes CLAIM folders into one $7.25B pen, with empty
   stalls marked +4 / +8 / +12 / +16 YEARS for future claims, and a WARNING gate the Supreme Court swings shut.
   It is strong on the settlement and on the brand pun. But it can't hold tonight's news (the order and Means), and
   herding claims reads close to herding the sick.
3. **The garden ad.** A borrowed 1990s weedkiller commercial where the weeds are lawsuits, sprayed by a settlement,
   a ruling and a verdict. It is the most surprising of the three, but the least true: a settlement pays people, it
   doesn't kill their claims. Rejected.

**Why the label:**
- It is truest. Durnell *is* about what goes on the label, the order is about the product, and Means's allegation is
  about who got a say. All three meet on one object.
- It is the most surprising. The product never speaks. Things are done to its packaging while the warning box stays
  empty, and the viewer reads the argument off a jug.

**Medium:** the 3D toy-world look (Cut First's grade) with a new subject, a shed workbench. It is close and tactile:
stickers, a stamp, a note, a phone. It is not the road kit.
