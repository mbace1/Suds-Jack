# Nordic / Doodle: the next five candidates (research, 19 Oct 2026)

Scope: Nordic and EU tech/AI stories from about 1–19 Oct 2026 for the 12:50 European window, plus Doodle
(Google) beyond what is already made. **Not repeated:** *Biodiversity Fund*, *On Hold* (Muhos/Kajaani),
*Satire Excepted* (Danish deepfake bill, including its 8 Oct introduction), *It Was Only a Test* (AI
Act's first named case), *Same Trick, Two Verdicts*.

**Method.** Web search only. Every page fetch failed: the proxy refused yle.fi, svt.se, qz.com,
helsinkitimes.fi and others. So each fact below comes from search-index excerpts of the named article,
cross-checked between outlets. **CONFIRMED** means two or more independent outlets, or the primary
source itself (a ministry, a municipality, the company), carry it. **SINGLE-SOURCE** means one outlet
only. **No verbatim quotes are given**, because no original article could be opened (owner rule: quote
only what was read in the original). Headlines are listed as titles, not as quotes. Last lines are the
station's own words.

**Disclosure for the editor.** This research was done by Claude, an Anthropic model. Two items touch AI
labs. In #2 the unnamed anchor customer is "one of two leading AI labs", and I have not guessed which.
In the Nscale reserve, Anthropic is reported as one of its two biggest contract holders.

**Naming.** Google stays **Doodle**. Other brands get a parody name on screen (suggestions below). Use
real people's names only where the story needs them; none of these five needs one.

## Ranked shortlist (best first)

1. **One Stockholm** (Doodle, Sweden, 14 Oct). Doodle's planned Torsboda data centre could draw about
   as much power as Stockholm, and on jobs it still says it is "too early". It is a clean question with
   a checkable answer, it fits the series, and it is not Finland again.
2. **No Names** (Jämsä, Finland, 8–13 Oct). There is a €10bn AI campus on a closed paper mill, and its
   backers name neither the customer nor the money. The grid company says it may ask for collateral.
   Finnish primary sources (Yle), and the freshest Finnish money story.
3. **Point Them at Nobody** (Norway, 5 Oct). Norway proposes the first government ban on AI glasses in
   named places: the beach, the gym changing room, the playground. The primary source is a ministry
   release, and the list draws itself. It does overlap with *You Looked At It*.
4. **Who Signs?** (Humppila + EK, Finland, late Sep–6 Oct). A town replaced two directors with "AI
   help", and industry now wants the Constitution's personal-liability rule widened. A strong Finnish
   civics question, but its theme sits close to *The Chair Is Taken*.
5. **How to Appeal** (Denmark, Aug → mid-Oct). Asked how to complain about a council decision, the
   chatbot shared by 40+ councils explains how to complain about a general election. The joke is the
   best of the five, but the facts are the oldest and the October hook is thin.

Reserves are at the end: Doodle Finland's apology and protests, Nscale Glomfjord, Norway's
foreign-owned data-centre capacity, the EU age-check app, and Swedish AI-written complaints.

## 1. One Stockholm (Doodle in Torsboda, Sweden)

**What happened.**
- **Summer 2026:** Google, through its company DSC International, agreed to buy the whole Torsboda
  Industrial Park (Timrå/Sundsvall) from the two municipalities to build a data centre.
- **17 Aug:** both councils approved the sale. The sale has conditions, and one of them is an
  electricity supply contract with E.ON.
- **Power, as the deal documents state it:** 650 MW in phase one and a target of 1,000 MW, with an
  option to expand to 1,800–2,000 MW. Google says the current talks cover up to 1 GW. Two outlets
  headlined the top figure as needing as much power as Stockholm (Energinyheter: more power than the
  city of Stockholm; Sveriges Radio: as much as all of Stockholm).
- **Jobs:** the "at least 500 direct jobs" figure comes from the **seller**, the municipal company, not
  from Google.
  - When Google met Sundsvall's municipal board on 5 Aug, the municipality's own release says Google
    stressed it was too early to say how many jobs there would be.
  - SVT reported that Google declined interviews and answered with a copy of an old press release.
  - SVT also reported that a Business Sweden report sees staffing per MW at AI data centres falling by
    up to 90–95%.
- **Price:** Svenska kraftnät told Sveriges Radio that adding a load like this without new generation
  would affect electricity prices across the whole country. It suggested requiring the developer to
  contribute its own generation.
  - The finance-markets minister called Google to a meeting on electricity supply on 17 Sep.
- **14 Oct (newest):** at a business forum in Sundsvall, Google said it is in the final phase of
  negotiations and a decision is due soon.
  - It said the site will be built out in stages, and that it will help with power supply through
    talks with producers.
  - It has not decided which new generation investments it will make, and it still gave no job figure.
  - The sale could complete in October if the conditions are met.

## 2. No Names (Sesterce in Jämsä, Finland)

**What happened.**
- **8 Oct 2026:** French AI-infrastructure firm Sesterce announced a campus of more than €10bn on the
  former UPM Kaipola paper mill site in Jämsä.
  - Capacity: a 200 MW first phase, rising to 600 MW, with a long-term aim of over 1 GW in Finland.
  - Jobs: about 2,000 during construction and about 300 permanent jobs.
  - A €10m local community fund, to be set up once the campus is in use.
  - A pledge of 1 MW of new renewable generation for every MW consumed, within 10 years.
- **The customer and the money:** the CEO told Yle that the investment decision is made and a contract
  is signed with a future customer. He said the customer is one of two leading AI labs but did not name
  it, and he declined to name the financial backers.
  - Further coverage: financing structure, loan or equity shares and payment schedule are not public.
  - The site sale from Kaipola Green Port is still being negotiated, and many permits remain.
- **The grid company:** the CEO of Elenia, which owns the two 110 kV lines into Kaipola, told Yle it will
  check Sesterce's background very carefully and ask for collateral (vakuus) if needed. Because the
  lines already exist, Elenia's own early risk is small. Fingrid still has a say.
- **The town:** the mayor stresses that nothing is settled yet. He has floated the €10m fund as useful
  for something like a new library.
- **13 Oct:** FAIR EDIH summarised it as announced while "site sale, permits and backers remain open"
  (its headline).

## 3. Point Them at Nobody (Norway's AI-glasses ban)

**What happened.**
- **5 Oct 2026:** Norway's Ministry of Digitalisation and Public Governance announced it will propose a
  **temporary ban on AI glasses in selected places**. The ministry's concern is people being filmed or
  recorded without knowing it.
- **The ministry's list:**
  - public areas: parks, beaches, museums, shopping centres and public events;
  - children's places: schools, kindergartens, playgrounds and youth clubs;
  - privacy-sensitive places: health institutions, and gyms with changing rooms and showers;
  - universities and colleges are to be considered.
- **What stays allowed:** private use, and use where nobody risks being filmed without consent.
- **Next steps:**
  - a bill to the Storting "as soon as possible" (reported wording);
  - an expert group to advise on permanent national rules;
  - which devices are covered (camera glasses, AI glasses, wider wearables) is still to be decided.
- **Context:**
  - Oslo schools and Equinor had already banned the glasses at some sites, and the gym chain Sats is
    considering its own rules.
  - The previous minister did not want a ban.
  - The FT called it the first such proposal by any government (reported second-hand).

## 4. Who Signs? (Humppila's AI directors and EK's constitution proposal, Finland)

**What happened.**
- **Late Sep 2026 (HS first):** Humppila, a municipality of about 2,000 people in Kanta-Häme,
  abolished its administrative director and technical director posts on the municipal manager's
  proposal.
  - The saving is about €150,000.
  - The technical director's work went to several staff. The manager does the administrative
    director's work herself with AI agents, according to her account.
  - AI drafts board decision proposals, contracts and tenders.
- **Oversight:** Deputy Parliamentary Ombudsman Susanna Lindroos-Hovinheimo opened an own-initiative
  inquiry into AI in municipal authority work. She is asking:
  - what tasks are given to AI;
  - how the lawfulness of decisions is secured;
  - how responsibility for computer-made decisions is resolved.

  She stresses there is no suspicion of misconduct. The former administrative director has appealed to
  the administrative court and filed a police report.
- **6 Oct:** EK (the Confederation of Finnish Industries) proposed amending **Constitution §118**, the
  personal liability of officials. It wants a new provision on **organisational and management
  responsibility** alongside personal liability, so AI can be used more widely in public
  administration.
  - EK's argument: today every decision must have an individual official behind it, and automated
    decisions are allowed only where there is no discretion (the 2023 law). EK calls that an
    unnecessary limit.
  - The proposal rests on an opinion by Vaasa public-law professor Niina Mäntylä.
  - JUKO, the public-sector professionals' union, says AI is not yet fit for decisions on citizens'
    rights. It asks whether a constitutional change is needed at all, but calls the model
    "interesting".
  - Yle corrected its first headline, which had said AI could decide on social assistance.

**The one question.** *If AI does the town director's job, who answers for its decisions?*

**Three facts.**
1. Humppila cut two director posts. The work is now done "with AI help", saving €150,000.
2. In Finland every official decision has a personally liable official, and the Ombudsman is now asking
   who that is.
3. On 6 Oct, industry proposed adding "organisational responsibility" to the Constitution for AI.

**Punchline (station's words).** "Every decision needs a name. Industry suggests: 'the organisation'." (9 words)

**Four shots (papercut).**

## 5. How to Appeal (Denmark's shared council chatbot)

**What happened.**
- **The stakes:** the government, KL (the municipalities) and Danish Regions have set a goal that AI
  should **free at least 30,000 full-time posts** in the public sector by 2035. It is in the new
  government platform (June 2026). The minister says it is about freeing staff, not firing them, and
  the Finance Ministry itself notes large uncertainty about AI's potential.
- **The chatbot:** Muni is the citizen chatbot run by Aarhus and shared by 40+ municipalities (KL lists
  43).
  - A journalist from Extra Fokus tested it in June 2026.
  - Asked what rights you have after a home-help application is refused, it answered with the rules for
    **resident deposit loans** (beboerindskudslån).
  - Asked how to complain about a municipal decision, it consistently said you can complain about **a
    general election** (folketingsvalg).
- **16 Aug:** the Parliamentary Ombudsman opened inquiries into AI projects in six municipalities and
  one ministry, Aarhus/Muni among them. They cover legal basis, testing, human oversight and duties of
  guidance. Replies were due within eight weeks, around mid-October. No outcome has been published.
- **October hooks:**
  - The Danish Council for Data Ethics warned on 13 Oct against broad AI powers for authorities.
  - The data protection authority answered the revised public-sector AI authorisation bill around
    12–15 Oct.
