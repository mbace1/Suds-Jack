## 4. Face recognition and the privacy wording (the core)

### 4a. What Apple's own text says

These lines appeared in quote marks in several excerpts. Verify them against the newsroom page before quoting.

1. **The gating rule** (Apple Newsroom, also carried by Mac Observer and MacRumors, 13 Oct): "content in apps like Messages and Calendar is only surfaced when the enrolled user is recognized by their face, and when their paired iPhone or iPad is present and has been recently unlocked."
2. **The camera and microphone pipeline** (Apple release, quoted by Mac Observer, MacRumors, Gotechtor and ID Tech): "Data that is processed and analyzed in Secure Exclaves cannot be accessed by the HomeView software, apps, the user, or Apple."
   - Apple's product page (apple.com/in/apple-homeview/) puts it slightly differently: the A18's Secure Exclaves "process the camera and microphone feeds in a secure compartment that cannot be accessed by the operating system or by Apple." That wording appeared once, so treat the exact wording as SINGLE-SOURCE.
3. **The cloud.** Siri requests run either on the device or in Private Cloud Compute, where Apple says data is not stored nor made accessible to Apple or anyone else. This is Apple's standard Private Cloud Compute line.
   - Sources: MacRumors (13 Oct) and the search summaries of the release. Several excerpts reproduced the wording but not in quote marks, so I am treating it as paraphrase.
4. **Wi-Fi exception footnote.** Personal information may stay available after the paired phone drops off Wi-Fi if four things hold:
   - HomeView still recognises the face;
   - the phone was recently on the same Wi-Fi network;
   - the phone has cellular service and is in the same location;
   - the phone was recently unlocked.
   - Sources: MacRumors, macrumors.com/2026/10/13/homeview-touts-5-privacy-features/ (13 Oct). Mac Observer, macobserver.com/news/homeview-personal-content-privacy/ (n/d). heise (n/d).
5. **"Recently" is not defined anywhere.**
   - Sources: The Hack Academy (n/d). Mac Observer excerpt.
   - Two outlets made this observation, and neither found a definition in Apple's text.

### 4b. The "not stored on the device once you leave" line: check before quoting

- **Who said it.** It comes from an Apple **product marketing manager, Cecilia Dantas**, speaking to **ABC** (ABC News / GMA plus the ABC-owned stations; abc7news.com/story/what-know-new-apple-homeview-smart-home-display-hub/19937996/; abcnews.com/GMA/News/…story?id=137234817; ~13 Oct).
- **What she said.** Excerpts carry these fragments in quote marks:
  - "recognizes your face and adapts to everyone who's using it"
  - "then, it pulls information from your iPhone"
  - It can recognise up to six users.
- **The storage clause** only reached me in paraphrase: **once you leave, your data is not stored on the device.**
  - A search for the exact phrase *your data is not stored on the device* returned the ABC pages as its top hits, which suggests the phrase is on those pages.
  - I could not read those pages, so **the verbatim wording is not confirmed and is SINGLE-SOURCE (ABC).** Do not put it in quote marks on screen until someone has watched or read the ABC segment.
- **The same idea from other outlets.**
  - **Reuters** (Stephen Nellis, 13 Oct; reprinted by KSL, The Star, RTÉ and Investing.com) reports that HomeView draws on a **personal search index stored on the user's iPhone**, and that the phone has to be on the same network.
  - **Bluewin** (Swiss) says personal contextual data is not stored directly on HomeView.
  - So the substance has three independent outlets (ABC, Reuters, bluewin). The *exact wording* has only one.
- **Important scope note:**
  - **The "not stored" promise is about your *personal data*** (Messages, Calendar, personal context). It is **not** a stated promise about your **face**.
  - Apple's announcement **does not say where face-enrolment data is kept, in what form, or for how long.** ID Tech Wire and the Hack Academy explainer both say so. Several of my searches for "where are HomeView face templates stored / does it sync via iCloud" found nothing.
  - What Apple *does* say about faces is an **access** claim (Secure Exclaves: nobody can access the data), not a **retention** claim.

### 4c. Other facts about the face system

- **Up to six enrolled members.** Each needs an Apple Account and a compatible iPhone or iPad, and is enrolled through the Home app.
  - Sources: Tom's Guide, MacRumors, ABC, Martin Cid Magazine headline ("knows six faces at home"), Mac Observer how-to (quoting the Apple Store FAQ: "you can have up to six members of the household enrolled on Apple HomeView through the Home app").
- **Your phone has to vouch for your face.** Recognition needs a **"registered personal device"** with Apple Intelligence / Siri AI, present on the home Wi-Fi and recently unlocked. **An Apple Watch does not count.** The same applies to voice recognition.
  - Sources: Daring Fireball (Gruber, 13 Oct), 9to5Mac (13 Oct), Techmeme.
  - Device floor of iPhone 15 Pro or newer: iPhone in Canada (13 Oct). Treat that floor as SINGLE-SOURCE.
- **Apple's rationale.** Apple reps told Gruber the home is presumed to be a safe space. That is Gruber's paraphrase, so it is SINGLE-SOURCE (Daring Fireball, echoed by 9to5Mac).
- **AppleInsider's headline:** "No biometrics are needed for HomeView to show a user's personal data" (13 Oct).

## 5. A 7th person, guests and visitors

- **Unrecognised people get the shared layer.** They can control accessories, check the weather, set timers, play music and see shared photo albums. They do not see anyone's Messages or Calendar.
  - Sources: AppleInsider (13 Oct), iThinkDiff (n/d), Macworld (shared albums are open to guests), Mac Observer how-to.
- **There is no manual guest mode.** As far as reported, the guest view kicks in automatically. Google Nest offers a manual Guest Mode for comparison.
  - Sources: search synthesis of Mac Observer and iThinkDiff. Weak: the claim rests on no outlet mentioning a manual mode.
- **A 7th household member is not named as a case anywhere.**
  - Read off the cap of six, they would sit in the guest layer. That is my **inference**, not a reported fact.
- **What happens to a visitor's or seventh person's face** (processed? discarded? kept?) **is not stated** in anything I could find.
  - Amazon's equivalent states it does not keep images or vectors of unenrolled people; see section 10.
- **Network trap.** A family member whose phone sits on the *guest* Wi-Fi may not be recognised.
  - Sources: Wi-Fi Planet / search synthesis. SINGLE-SOURCE.
- **HomeView + compatible cameras** (doorbells and so on) offer end-to-end-encrypted recognition of "people, familiar faces, pets, packages, and vehicles" with an iCloud+ plan. In other words, **visitors' faces are a feature on the camera side.**
  - Sources: MacRumors 5-privacy-features post (13 Oct). Apple Newsroom "Apple Home Collection" release (apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/introducing-apple-home-collection-…).
  - The quoted list is SINGLE-SOURCE for its exact wording.

## 6. Is the camera always on?

- **Macworld's HomeView guide** describes HomeView as having "an always-available camera and microphones in a shared household space". It says there is **"no physical camera/microphone privacy switch"** and that both can be turned off in software.
  - Source: Macworld, macworld.com/article/676817/…
- **A second Macworld piece** (review, macworld.com/article/3261040/…) also flags the missing hardware switch.
- Both are Macworld, so **treat this as SINGLE-OUTLET.** No second outlet confirmed it in the excerpts I got.
- **Indirect support from a second outlet.** MacRumors says the **Flow** face reacts to presence and movement, so *some* sensing is live while the screen is idle. MacRumors does not say which sensor does it.
- **Comparison:** Google's Nest Hub Max had a hardware kill switch that physically cut both the camera and the mic.
  - Source: Fast Company (older piece). SINGLE-SOURCE.
- **No source mentioned a camera indicator light.**

## 10. Precedents

### Ring "Familiar Faces" (Amazon), launched December 2025

- **What it does.** Owners can build a library of **up to 50 faces**. The feature is **off by default**.
  - Sources: Ring support page (ring.com/support/articles/z3yhg/familiar-faces); TechCrunch (9 Dec 2025).
- **Unnamed faces** are auto-deleted after **30 days** without a recognition.
  - Sources: Ring support page; TechCrunch (9 Dec 2025); CyberGuy.
- **Blocked areas.** It is unavailable in **Illinois, Texas, Portland (OR) and Quebec** because of local law.
  - Sources: Ring support page; EFF; CyberGuy; Biometric Update (Nov 2025).
- **EFF's objection.** When the feature is on, it scans **everyone** who comes into view (relatives, delivery drivers, children selling cookies) without their consent.
  - Sources: EFF (eff.org/node/111375); TechRepublic; Biometric Update.
- **Sen. Ed Markey** wrote to Amazon in Oct 2025 and got a reply in Nov 2025 (markey.senate.gov PDF). In Feb 2026 he urged Amazon to drop the feature altogether (Markey press release).
- **Class action.** Charles Sigwalt filed a class action in Seattle in **June 2026**, alleging passers-by are scanned and stored without consent. Outcome unknown.
  - Sources: TechCrunch (2 Jun 2026); The Register (3 Jun 2026).

### Ring "Search Party" Super Bowl ad, February 2026

- **What happened.** Ring's lost-dog ad, built on the neighbourhood's cameras, drew heavy backlash: EFF called it a "surveillance nightmare", and Markey and the ACLU also objected. Ring then **ended its planned Flock Safety partnership**.
  - Sources: CNBC (12 Feb 2026); Variety; CBS News; TechRadar; Techdirt (23 Feb 2026).
- **Contested:** Ring says the ad was **not** why it ended the partnership; it cited integration cost. Sources: ABC7 LA; Ring statement.
- **Why it matters:** this is the closest precedent for a cosy family advert that turns out to be about surveillance.

### Amazon Echo Show "Visual ID", 2021

- **How it works.** It is opt-in, and enrolled faces are stored **on the device**. Amazon says it **does not keep images or vectors of unenrolled people** after processing. An ID that goes unrecognised for 18 months is auto-deleted.
  - Sources: Amazon Science blog ("The science behind visual ID"); CNET 2021, via a mirror (verify the original).
- **The contrast:** Amazon published a policy for strangers' faces. **Apple's HomeView materials, as reported, do not.**

## (a) Insight

The record shows Apple's spoken promise that your data is not stored on HomeView "once you leave" covers the personal data pulled from your iPhone. Apple has published no storage or retention terms for the faces it recognises, or for a visitor's face. And Apple's own launch film ends with the family using the device's camera to watch their daughter leave.

## (b) The joke: three premises for a 25 s clay parody

**Working rename:** **"Abble HomeWho"** (or "HomeVyy"). The device is a dome with a screen on a stubby neck, which reads as a desk pet. Set design: a clay kitchen island with a hole drilled through the middle.

### Premise 1: "The Bouncer"

The device only knows six faces, and your face only counts if your phone vouches for it.

1. **Embroidery hoop.** Clay hands stitch a rainbow *pear*, with the caption "Welcome home."
2. **Morning kitchen**, French toast smoking. The HomeWho has grown a velvet rope and a clipboard of six face photos. Each family member steps up, shows their face, then **waves an unlocked phone like a club wristband**. A green tick lands each time.
3. **Grandma arrives in person** (the screen still shows her on FaceTime). She is not on the clipboard and holds a flip phone. The screen shows only a big **WEATHER** icon.
4. **She smiles hopefully. It shows her a timer.**
5. **Wide shot:** the family of six-plus-one at the island. The device's eye follows Grandma.

Closing card: **"Welcome home. Show ID."**

### Premise 2: "Once You Leave"

This uses the ad's own ending. The promise is about what is forgotten when you leave; the film's climax is watching someone leave.

1. **Hoop:** "Welcome home." is stitched, and then **"(once you leave)"** is added in smaller thread.
2. **Kitchen chaos at double speed.** The son sprays whipped cream into his mouth, the siblings tug the thermostat, and the device's **Flow face swirls toward whoever moves**.
3. **The daughter tiptoes out of the door.** The device's dome swivels like an owl.
4. **The whole family crowds round the 7-inch screen** watching the doorbell feed of the daughter and her boyfriend: nine tiles, all of the boyfriend. Dad eats French toast like popcorn.
5. **The boyfriend looks straight into the lens.** On screen: **"GUEST 7: ?"**

Closing card: **"It forgets you. Not your guests?"**

The question mark matters. Apple's handling of guests' faces is undisclosed, not proven bad, so the card must ask, not assert.

### Premise 3: "Remote Control for an iPhone"

The hub is a $299 screen that only knows you because your phone tells it.

1. **Launch pose:** a clay HomeWho on a plinth lit like a product shot. The rotating turntable is visibly hand-cranked.
2. **Mum stands in front of it. Nothing happens.** It looks past her to her handbag.
3. **She digs out her phone and unlocks it.** Instantly the screen floods with her Messages and Calendar, and she beams.
4. **She sets the phone down and walks two steps away.** The screen still shows everything, because the Wi-Fi exception footnote covers it. The brother leans in.
5. **She snatches the phone back.** The device wilts like a sad pet with its cable down the island hole.

Closing card: **"Your iPhone, with a face."**

## (c) Risks and contested points

- **Rename the brand on screen.**
  - Use "Abble" or "Pear" and "HomeWho" or "HomeVyy", the same move as Google → "Doodle" and Tesla → "Teslur".
  - No Apple logo. The embroidered **rainbow Apple logo is a trademark**, so stitch a rainbow *pear* instead.
  - No "Liquid Retina", no "Siri" (name the assistant something else), and **no Snoopy face** (Peanuts IP).
  - Do not reuse the real film's footage, soundtrack or exact title card. Copy the *look* (island, cord hole, family of five, morning light), not the frames.
- **No real faces.**
  - The actors in Apple's film are real private people, so build generic clay characters.
  - Do not depict Greg Joswiak, Cecilia Dantas, Gruber, MKBHD or Benedict Evans.
  - If an exec-style presenter is needed, make it an anonymous clay figure.
- **Do not caption "not stored once you leave" in quote marks** until someone has heard or read the ABC segment. Its verbatim wording is SINGLE-SOURCE and unverified here.
  - Do not imply Apple *keeps* face data. The record says the terms are **unstated**, not that the data is retained.
  - Frame it as a question, as in Premise 2's card.
