## 4. The toaster vs the frontier model: what is actually binding
### Toaster, United States
- **UL 1026** (Electric Household Cooking and Food Serving Appliances) covers household
  cooking appliances rated **250 V or less**, toasters included. The standard names "toaster" as
  an appliance with its own specific requirements. Sources: Intertek
  https://intertek.com/standards-updates/ul-1026-electrical-household-cooking-food-serving-appliances ·
  UL explore-standards page https://explorestandards.ul.org/cooking-and-serving-appliances.html ·
  GlobalSpec. **Confirmed.**
- Revisions to the 6th edition (2016–17) added **a redundant control, based on a food-load
  ignition test**, and **a secondary operating control for cord-connected automatic toasters**.
  In practice that means a second control that stops the toaster if the first one fails. A 2021
  revision (effective March 2023) added rules against accidental switch-on for touch-control
  toasters. Sources: InCompliance https://incompliancemag.com/ul-1026-standard-electric-household-cooking-appliances ·
  Intertek China https://intertek.com.cn/Readingen_itemid_6526.html · Intertek notice
  https://cdn.intertek.com/www-intertek-com/dms-legacy/UL-1026-SUN-Rev-3-19-2021-ED-3-19-2023.pdf.
  **Confirmed** (two independent outlets: InCompliance and Intertek). This is the film's best
  fact: **the toaster standard already requires a backup shut-off.**
- **Caveat:** no federal law requires UL listing for a plug-in toaster. Listing is close to
  universal because of retailers, insurers and electrical codes, but it is voluntary. OSHA
  recognises testing labs (NRTLs) for workplaces. Sources: Residential Systems
  https://www.residentialsystems.com/features/safety-ul-and-you · Mike Holt forums. These are
  trade/forum sources: **VERIFY** with CPSC before saying it on air.
- **What is binding:** **Consumer Product Safety Act §15(b)** (15 U.S.C. §2064(b);
  16 CFR Part 1115). Manufacturers, importers, distributors and retailers must tell the CPSC
  **"immediately"**, which the CPSC reads as **within 24 hours**, once they have information
  that reasonably supports the conclusion that a product has a defect that could create a
  substantial hazard. CPSC guidance: "When in doubt, report." Sources: CPSC
  https://www.cpsc.gov/node/12776 · Foley https://www.foley.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Consumer-Product-Safety-Act-Swanholt-McGaver-Sikora.pdf ·
  InCompliance May 2023 · Stinson. **Confirmed.**

### Toaster, European Union
- The **Low Voltage Directive 2014/35/EU** covers electrical equipment rated **50–1000 V AC /
  75–1500 V DC**. A toaster meeting it (and the other directives that apply) carries the **CE
  mark**. The manufacturer declares conformity; there is no compulsory third-party test. Sources:
  BAuA https://www.baua.de/EN/Topics/Work-design/Safe-products/Electrical-products/Requirements/1-ProdSV ·
  BSI · Element. **Confirmed.**
- **IEC 60335-2-9** (EU version **EN IEC 60335-2-9:2023+A11:2023**, used together with
  **IEC 60335-1**) covers portable household appliances with a cooking function rated **≤250 V**:
  toasters, contact grills, waffle irons, breadmakers. Sources: IEC webstore
  https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/61318 · AFNOR · SIS · EVS. **Confirmed.** Whether EN
  60335-2-9 is currently cited in the Official Journal under the LVD is **mixed**: an older edition
  was cited with restrictions (Decision (EU) 2017/1357). **VERIFY** before naming it on screen as
  "the harmonised standard".
- **General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988**, in force since **13 Dec 2024**: a
  manufacturer must report any accident causing death or serious harm **without undue delay**,
  through the **Safety Business Gateway**. This applies to all consumer products. Sources: Cooley
  20 Dec 2024 https://products.cooley.com/2024/12/20/the-eus-general-product-safety-regulation-new-accident-reporting-obligations/ ·
  Baker McKenzie · Fieldfisher · Commission guidelines C/2025/6238. **Confirmed.**
- If the film uses Smith's washing machine instead: UL 2157 / CSA C22.2 No. 169 (5th ed.,
  30 Oct 2024) covers electric clothes washing machines (ANSI webstore, Intertek, CSA Group).
  **Confirmed.**

## (a) One-sentence insight
As of 17 Oct 2026, the record shows three things side by side. A US toaster maker must report a
hazardous defect to the CPSC within 24 hours, and the toaster standard (UL 1026) asks for a
second control in case the first fails. Federal US rules for frontier AI are voluntary by their
own text. And California's AI kill switch, a requirement Newsom vetoed in 2024, currently exists
as a recommendation due 16 Nov.

## (d) Risks and fairness
- **Dates:** the order is from **18 Sept**, not this week. The Al Jazeera "17 Oct" date is the
  article's, not the order's. The deadline is 16 Nov (Al Jazeera alone says 15 Nov).
- **Do not over-claim:** the order is a **study and recommendations, not a mandate**. Fox
  Business ("mandate") and Bloomberg Government ("requiring") headlines overstate it. Do not
  copy that framing.
- **"Toaster" is ours, not Smith's.** He said washing machine and milk. Either use the washing
  machine for any beat that carries his name, or keep the toaster clearly as the station's own
  example. Never put "toaster" inside his quote marks.
- **"More rules than AI" is true only with qualifiers.** It holds for **US federal, binding,
  pre-release** rules. It is false for the EU (AI Act Art. 55 has been enforceable since Aug 2026)
  and only partly true for California (SB 53 already requires 15-day incident reporting). On-screen
  text should say "US federal". The toaster side has its own caveat: UL listing is voluntary in the
  US. The binding duty is CPSA reporting and recalls. In the EU, CE marking is binding but
  self-declared.
- **Smith has a stake.** Microsoft builds and sells AI, and he also warned against heavy-handed
  rules (VERIFY). Showing his quote without saying who he works for would be one-sided. Say
  "Microsoft's president".
- **Newsom framing:** the 2028 angle comes from Al Jazeera's framing. His term ends Jan 2027.
  Keep it out of the film or attribute it.
