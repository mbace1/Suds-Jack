# Japan stories for Kyoto Toko and Tokyo Toko: research file

Compiled Mon 19 Oct 2026, for 20–30 s vertical films with the Japanese personas (`plan/toko-personas.md`).
The brief: real, recent Japan stories about AI or automation in daily life, ideally ones that can land in **both**
places on the same day (gathering dust at the farmhouse, taking over the 1K flat), or that suit one persona alone.

## Ranked shortlist (best first)

1. **Eight in Ten.** The government wants 80% of people using generative AI. Its own white paper puts use at 58.8%, and on 1 Oct the PM ordered an action plan by year-end. This is the purest form of *you still decide*. It is a twin film, and the survey was done **online**, so the farmhouse was never asked. CONFIRMED.
2. **No Driver.** On 15 Sep, Waymo, GO and Nihon Kotsu announced fully driverless taxis for Tokyo in 2027. Meanwhile the one bus over the pass beyond **Ohara**, in Kyoto Toko's own hamlet country, was scrapped for lack of drivers. Each half of the twin is a real story. *The people behind the machine.* CONFIRMED.
3. **Twenty-Four Hours / Mornings Only.** Shizuoka put a 24-hour, 365-day LINE AI in front of 18,500 public-housing tenants. The pilot started **16 Oct**, three days ago. The 24/7 promise sits against Kyoto Toko's 電話は午前中のみ, and the AI hands off to a human counsellor. It is the freshest story and the best twin image, but the subject is the most delicate. CONFIRMED (primary).
4. **The Answer Was Drafted.** Kasumigaseki's in-house AI, Gennai, now reaches about 180,000 officials and is being readied to draft Diet answers, because that work ran late into the night. A human still checks every line. It suits Tokyo Toko alone; Kyoto Toko can hear the answer on his transistor radio. CONFIRMED.
5. **491.** In a year, AI-assisted entries to Nikkei's Hoshi Shinichi Award rose from 93 to 491, and AI helped 3 of the 4 general-category winners. The twin is brush and ink against a phone on the last train. *Go make your own.* CONFIRMED (primary), but 8 months old.
6. **More Apologies Than People.** In 2026, leaked personal data reached a cumulative 165 million+ people's worth, in a country of about 122 million. A security firm thinks AI is driving the attacks. The twin: 99+ apology emails in the flat, one postcard at the farmhouse. The message fit is the weakest of the six. CONFIRMED.

Reserves (short notes at the end): the **Buddharoid** robot monk at a Kyoto temple (Kyoto Toko only, Feb 2026);
**AI bear-detection cameras** (Kyoto Toko only, Sep 2026, deadly context); the **false M9.9 earthquake alert** of
16 Oct (automation, very fresh, life-safety: probably do not make).

## Method, and what it can't vouch for

- **Every page fetch failed.** WebFetch returned DNS errors (pref.shizuoka.jp, japantimes.co.jp, kaiketsu-j.com) or refused the host (nhk.or.jp). **Every claim below therefore comes from search-engine excerpts** of the pages named, not from pages I read in full. Re-read the primary page before anything goes on screen.
- **Quoted text.** Japanese in 「brackets」 is wording that appeared in the search excerpt of the named page. It is near-verbatim, **not** checked against the full page. No English quote marks are used for anyone's speech, and no quote is attributed to a real person beyond what is reported.
- **Labels.** **CONFIRMED** means two or more independent outlets, or a primary source (a government, company or award-organiser release). **SINGLE-SOURCE** means one outlet. Kyodo copy carried by Sakigake and Kumanichi counts as **one** outlet (Kyodo), and so does Jiji copy on nippon.com and adnkronos.
- **Staging is not sourcing.** In a twin film the same news "arrives" at the farmhouse and the flat. That is the personas' stage. On-screen captions must carry only what the sources say, and no prop may pretend to be a real government flyer, crest or letter.

## 1. Eight in Ten: the government's 80% AI-use target

**What happened**
- **24 Jul 2026.** The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) published the 2026 *Information and Communications White Paper* (令和8年版 情報通信白書). It found:
  - **58.8%** of people in Japan have used generative AI, about double the 26.7% of the year before. The survey ran Jan–Feb 2026.
  - About **3 in 10** users use it almost every day (29.9%).
  - By age: **15–19: 91.7%**, **20s: 78.2%**, **30s: 56.3%**, **40s: 49.0%**, **50s: 44.2%**, **60s: 33.5%**.
  - The commonest precaution users take is checking the answer some other way (**38.2%**).
  - The survey was an **internet survey** in four countries, with about 1,200 respondents in Japan. It covered ages 15–69. The 15–19 band is new this year, so the year-on-year doubling needs care.
- **Dec 2025.** Japan's first AI Basic Plan (人工知能基本計画) was approved by cabinet on **23 Dec 2025**. Reports on the draft (Jiji, 5 Dec 2025) said it aimed to lift individual generative-AI use to **50% first and 80% eventually**.
- **1 Oct 2026.** At the 8th Japan Growth Strategy Council, PM Takaichi said the government will compile an **action plan to spread AI use by year-end**. Jiji reports she called AI an accelerator (加速装置) of growth, and ministers were told to draft the AI picture and the regulatory reforms.

## 2. No Driver: Tokyo's driverless taxis, and the Ohara bus that lost its driver

**What happened**
- **15 Sep 2026, Tokyo.** Waymo, the taxi app GO and the cab company Nihon Kotsu said they aim to launch **Japan's first fully driverless commercial taxi service in Tokyo in 2027**:
  - It starts small and grows to **about 100 vehicles** (Automotive World says Jaguar I-Pace).
  - Rides will be booked through both apps.
  - Public-road tests have run in **seven Tokyo wards since 2025**, still with a Nihon Kotsu crew aboard.
  - Nihon Kotsu runs the depot and carries legal responsibility.
  - No launch date has been set; it depends on approvals.
- **Kyoto.** On **13 Feb 2026**, Kyoto Bus announced it would abolish **route 10, the Hira line** (出町柳駅前 → 大原 → 朽木学校前), on **15 Mar 2026**:
  - The given reason was 「深刻な運転士不足の影響」 (the effect of a severe driver shortage).
  - The route was about **43 km**, and the only bus from Ohara over the Saba-kaidō pass. It ran one round trip a day on spring-to-autumn weekends; in practice a hikers' bus.
  - Its last run was in Dec 2025, before the winter suspension, which then never ended.
  - It was one of several Kyoto-area cuts that spring blamed on the driver shortage, alongside Keihan Bus lines.

## 3. Twenty-Four Hours / Mornings Only: Shizuoka's always-on AI for public-housing tenants

**What happened**
- **6 Oct 2026.** Shizuoka Prefecture announced a pilot for its prefectural housing (県営住宅) under the name **AIよりそいサポート事業**.
- **How it works:**
  - Tenants message their housing questions and everyday troubles to **LINE**.
  - A generative AI answers in conversation, **24 hours a day, 365 days a year**, in Japanese, Portuguese, Filipino and English.
  - The AI sorts what it is told. When needed, **specialist counsellors** take over and connect people to municipal welfare services.
- **Who and when:**
  - All tenants: **126 estates, about 10,000 households, about 18,500 people**.
  - **16 Oct 2026 to 31 Mar 2027**, with formal adoption targeted for FY2027 if it works.
- **Why:**
  - Single elderly households are **36.4%** of tenants (1 Apr 2026), up 13.5 points in a decade.
  - The stated aim is to reduce isolation for elderly, foreign-national and child-raising tenants alike.
  - The release says it draws on a venture company's know-how. The company is unnamed in what I saw.
- **Context:** Yamagata City has run a LINE consultation where residents choose between a listening generative AI (24-hour) and a human counsellor, and a startup (つながりAI) runs a 24/365 AI partner for Yamagata municipal-housing tenants. So this is a pattern, not a one-off.

## 4. The Answer Was Drafted: Gennai, the government's AI, and the Diet's late nights

- **What Gennai is.** The Digital Agency built 源内 (Gennai), an in-house generative-AI platform for national civil servants.
  - It was built in-house so it can handle some confidential material.
  - Its functions are drafting, translation, meeting transcription and auto-sorting public comments.
- **The rollout:**
  - A large pilot opened to about **100,000 staff on 29 May 2026**.
  - On 20 Aug, Kyodo reported that the trial had grown to **about 180,000 staff**, regional offices included.
  - **A Diet-answer drafting function is to come**, with full use targeted for **FY2027**.

- **Concerns.** Kyodo notes over-reliance blunting policy understanding, and AI misinformation. Gendai Business ran a critical piece on why Gennai is 'falling flat'.

## 5. 491: AI-assisted entries flood a Japanese literary prize

**What happened**
- **20 Feb 2026.** Nikkei announced the results of the **13th Hoshi Shinichi Award** (日経「星新一賞」), a short-story prize for science fiction. Entries were taken 1 Jul – 7 Oct 2025.
- **The numbers:**
  - **2,107** entries, up 450 on the 1,657 of the year before.
  - Entries **recognised as partly AI-created rose from 93 to 491**. That is about 23% of entries, my arithmetic.
  - In the general category, **3 of 4 winners, the Grand Prix included**, used AI in the creative process.
- **The rules allow it.** The prize accepts entries "from non-humans such as AI", but it requires a record of the AI and prompts used, and human rewriting of generated text.
- **Secondary sources add:**
  - The Grand Prix 「ゲノムの塔」 used AI only to invent things like fictional disease names.
  - Half of the ten finalists were AI collaborations.
  - Nikkei ran a follow-up on 8 Mar 2026 (星新一賞、AI作品が上位独占 文学とは何かを問う), which I did not read.
- **Side note (SINGLE-SOURCE).** The 14th Hayakawa SF Contest (2026) drew 1,012 entries, and one site blames AI for the surge. Do not use it without a second source.
