## Coxon: US AI politics before 3 Nov (00:50)

### Per Caller · peg 19 Oct · High
**Q:** Can an AI call your cellphone about the election without asking?
1. A political group wants AI election calls to cellphones without consent. *CONFIRMED*
2. Its offered cap: three calls per 30 days, per caller. *CONFIRMED*
3. FCC replies closed 19 Oct; a ruling could come before 3 Nov. *CONFIRMED*

**Punchline:** The proposed limit: three AI calls a month. Per caller.
**Visual:** A kitchen table with an unsigned CONSENT form, and a phone that lights up UNKNOWN. Outside, masked callers
queue round the block, each holding a 3. **PHYS** carries the twist: calls land on the table one by one until the
pile shoves the phone off the edge.
**Risk:** The film could read as "allowed" when the change is only requested. Political robocalls to landlines are
already legal without consent. **Re-check:** whether the FCC has ruled, and the exact terms in DA 26-940. Present the
queue as the film's own inference, because the point that every PAC counts as its own caller is an opponent's argument. Keep party colours
off screen.

### That's a Wednesday · peg 5 Oct, holds to 3 Nov · High
**Q:** Should you ask a chatbot when to vote?
1. About one voter in five asks chatbots about the election. *SINGLE-SOURCE (via an aggregator)*
2. September test: 29% of voting answers incomplete, wrong or out of date. *CONFIRMED*
3. One chatbot said Election Day is 4 November. It is 3 November. *CONFIRMED*

**Punchline:** One chatbot said November fourth. That's a Wednesday.
**Visual:** A fortune-teller booth prints NOV 4. On Wednesday the gym is locked and the I VOTED stickers are in the
gutter. **MARK:** a red marker strikes WED 4 and circles TUE 3. The end card points to the county election office.
**Risk:** Name no chatbot and no lab. Disclosure: Claude made one of the errors in the California test, and the date
error was another firm's model. Say "in a September test"; the 29% includes incomplete answers, so never say "lie".
Imply no motive. **Re-check:** the date error's wording in the ISD PDF; the one-in-five figure against the AAPC
Foundation's release.

### Label Sold Separately · peg 16 Oct · High (handle with care)
**Q:** Would you know a political ad was made with AI?
1. By mid-October, $137M+ spent airing about 220 AI-made political ads. *CONFIRMED*
2. In the whole 2024 cycle: under $50,000. *SINGLE-SOURCE*
3. About 3 in 10 carried an AI label. *CONFIRMED*

**Punchline:** Ten AI ads. About three of them said so.
**Visual:** A wall of ten paper TVs. An aide sticks MADE WITH AI on three of them and pockets the sheet, seven labels
still on it. **MARK:** the marker circles the three labelled screens, and seven stay bare.
**Risk:** Partisanship: 81% of the ads are Republican per Wesleyan; show no party, and cite it only as Wesleyan's
count. "AI-made" includes ads merely enhanced with AI, and an unlabelled ad is not always illegal. Keep cash imagery
out, so it does not echo *Paid For By*. **Re-check:** the latest Wesleyan count; the 27% in the originals.

### The Building Is on the Ballot · peg 3 Nov · Med-high
**Q:** Who gets to vote on AI this November?
1. On 3 November, 34 local data-centre measures go to voters. *CONFIRMED (counts vary, 28–39)*
2. Most reached the ballot through neighbours' signatures. *SINGLE-SOURCE (Ballotpedia)*
3. Only 11% of Americans would welcome an AI data centre locally. *CONFIRMED*

**Punchline:** AI isn't on the ballot. Its buildings are, 34 times.
**Visual:** A paper server block on a flatbed idles at a town-limits sign, waiting its turn. **PHYS:** 34 ballots
rain onto a paper map, and about half pile up on Ohio.
**Risk:** Not every measure is a ban. Put "data centres" on the ballot paper. No company villain. The theme is close to
*A Handful of People*, and it is another data-centre film. **Re-check:** the count on the posting day. Do not single out
Wilmington, which is in court.

## Nordic / Doodle: Nordic and EU tech (12:50)

**Q:** A Stockholm's worth of power for Doodle. How many jobs for Sweden?
1. Up to 2,000 MW: about as much power as Stockholm. *CONFIRMED*
2. 500 jobs is the seller's estimate; Doodle gives no number yet. *CONFIRMED*
3. The grid operator warns prices rise nationwide without new generation. *CONFIRMED*

**Punchline:** Power: one Stockholm. Jobs: too early to say.
**Visual:** A lit paper Stockholm, with a cable running north. **TURN:** the skyline folds into one windowless grey box
in a pine forest. Two fence signs stand beside it: 500 JOBS (OUR ESTIMATE) and TOO EARLY TO SAY.
**Risk:** 2,000 MW is the contract's ceiling, and the talks cover about 1 GW, so say "up to". Never say Doodle denied
the 500. The price warning is conditional. Keep it Swedish, and keep the look apart from *On Hold*. **Re-check:** an
E.ON contract, a Doodle job figure, or the sale closing; any one of them changes the punchline.

### No Names · peg 8–13 Oct · High (punchline detail SINGLE-SOURCE)
**Q:** Who is behind Finland's €10bn AI campus?
1. A €10bn+ AI campus is planned on a closed Finnish paper mill. *CONFIRMED*
2. A customer is signed. Neither it nor the backers are named. *CONFIRMED*

**Visual:** A forklift wheels a giant cheque up to a cold mill chimney. **MARK:** the marker circles the blank PAY TO

**Risk:** Never imply fraud; an anonymous forum thread alleges it, and it must not be used. The grid company's check

## The Brink: front-line tech, espionage, surveillance (12:50; sober, no tactics)

### Tailgated · peg 15–19 Oct · High
**Q:** Who decides how close a satellite may come to another?
1. A Russian military satellite approached a Finnish company's radar satellite. *CONFIRMED*
2. From 350 km to under 5 km by 16 Oct; no damage. *CONFIRMED (LeoLabs)*
3. No law sets how close one satellite may come to another. *CONFIRMED*

**Punchline:** Within five kilometres. Legal minimum distance: none.
**Visual:** A distance tag on a thread counts down 350 KM … 5 KM. **MARK:** in a thick book titled ORBIT: RULES OF THE
ROAD, the marker runs down blank pages and circles the only two words, DUE REGARD. Then the satellites fly nose to tail.
**Risk:** Never say "attacked", "hunted" or "spy satellite"; intent is unknown. Use only "within 5 km", because the
smaller figures conflict. It is a Finnish company's satellite, not Finland's. Leave out the escalation lines and any
joke at the company's expense. **Re-check:** any new approach or statement. A 00:50 X re-up is possible, since US
outlets covered it.

### Break Glass in Emergency · peg 16 Oct, vote pending · High
**Q:** When does a temporary law stop being temporary?
1. 2024: Finland passed a one-year temporary law for its closed eastern border. *CONFIRMED*
2. It has never been used. In 2025 it was extended to 2026. *CONFIRMED*
3. Proposed now: two more years, unchanged. A committee agreed on 16 Oct. *CONFIRMED*

**Punchline:** Never used. Still temporary. Proposed: until 2028. Change it to "Now until 2028" only after the vote
passes.
**Visual:** A snowy border gate. On the guardhouse wall, a dusty, unbroken BREAK GLASS case. **MARK:** the inspection
tag's year is struck out and rewritten by hand: 2025, 2026, 2028. Two small DISSENT cards rise at the committee desk.
**Risk:** The people at the border are not the subject: no asylum seekers and no crowd. Do not argue the threat. Use
the Act's name, not its nicknames, and keep party colours off. **Re-check:** the plenary date and result on the
morning of posting, and which consultation the Chancellor of Justice's point about thin reasons came from (leave the
Chancellor unnamed).

### Again Urged · forward peg 26–30 Oct (ITU board) · Med-high
**Q:** Who can make the GPS jamming stop?
1. Finland's aviation sees GPS interference around the clock (Traficom, April). *CONFIRMED*
2. Finland and the Baltics complained to the UN's radio body, the ITU. *CONFIRMED*
3. Its board urged Russia to stop, then again urged. No reply recorded. *CONFIRMED*

**Punchline:** Urged. Again urged. Reply on record: none.
**Visual:** On a paper cockpit screen, the plane icon jumps off the coast into a lake, and the pilot unfolds a chart.
**PHYS:** letters stamped URGE and AGAIN URGE pile up unopened beside NO RESPONSE RECEIVED.
**Risk:** Attribution: Finland and Lithuania trace the interference to Russia, and Russia denies it or calls it
self-protection; the post copy carries both. No crash talk, no explanation of how spoofing works, and keep the 18 Oct
drone out. Be fair to the ITU, which has few powers by design. **Re-check:** the meeting numbers in the minutes,
whether the case is on the 26 Oct agenda, and whether any reply has come.

### The Owners' Responsibility · peg 18 Oct · Med (live cases)
**Q:** Who guards Europe's satellite-internet ground stations?
1. Fires near Warsaw and Sofia; an attempt in Vilnius on 18 Oct. *CONFIRMED*
2. Poland and Lithuania call it Russian-linked sabotage; Moscow rejects the claims. *CONFIRMED*
3. Bulgaria's interior minister said site security lies with the owners. *CONFIRMED (read in the original)*

**Punchline:** Three ground stations. Security: the owners' responsibility.
**Visual:** A ring of paper dishes behind a low fence marked PRIVATE PROPERTY. Three pins go into a map. **MARK:** the
marker underlines OWNERS' on the minister's card, and the film cuts back to the low fence.
**Risk:** Show no method, ever. Nobody has been convicted, so show no suspects and give no nationalities. Bulgaria has
not attributed its case. The satellite company is not accused (use **Starlunk** if a brand must appear). Keep the tone
at prepared, not afraid, which is Supo's own message. **Re-check:** arrests or charges on the morning of posting, and the minister's exact words
(BTA or the original).

### Open, Sealed · peg 15 Oct · Med
**Q:** Can Finland seal your invention as a state secret?
1. Patent is from the Latin for open: you publish, the law protects. *UNCHECKED (etymology source not opened)*
2. A 15 Oct bill: defence inventions could be kept secret, not seized. *CONFIRMED*
3. The impact watchdog: business costs assessed only at a general level. *CONFIRMED*

**Punchline:** Patent means open. This one comes sealed.
**Visual:** A desk sign at the patent counter reads PATENT (LAT. OPEN), and the clerk carries the envelope through a
door marked DEFENCE. **TURN:** the open envelope folds itself shut under a red wax seal reading SECRET · 1 YEAR. The
gadget must not look like a weapon.
**Risk:** It is a bill. Defence secrecy is legitimate, and the bill is milder than the 1967 law (the owner keeps the
invention and is compensated). It is the lightest Brink idea. **Re-check:** the etymology; whether the draft's rule (a secret
application is sealed, with no patent) survived; which authority decides; whether an order can be renewed; entry into force (2027).

2. At least 45 computers at one newsroom were targeted this way. *CONFIRMED*
3. The firm was on the industry's vendor list until an October suspension. *CONFIRMED (exact date SINGLE-SOURCE)*

**Visual:** A cheerful banner ad slides into the corner of a laptop screen in a newsroom at night. **TURN:** the paper

### Land, Power, Water · peg 5 and 17 Oct · Med
