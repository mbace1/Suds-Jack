Owner, 17 Oct: "Go with 1, 2 and 3." These are the three pitches in the `PLANNING.md` template. Each draws
on a research pack beside this file (`research-chair.md`, `research-killswitch.md`,
`research-welcome.md`).

**Two warnings before anything is drawn.** No article page could be opened from this sandbox, so every fact
and every quote comes from search excerpts that were cross-checked between outlets. Each on-screen quote must
be checked against its original page before air. **Disclosure:** the research was done with Claude, an
Anthropic model. Anthropic appears in pitch 1 (one of the four companies that gave no number on 5 Oct) and
in the background to pitch 2 (with OpenAI, in the test-escape incidents). Both companies are treated alike.

Look tests: `looks/kill-switch-leaflet.png` (the new safety-leaflet look) and `looks/welcome-home-clay.png`

## 1. The Chair Is Taken · RFH · Coxon #5 · papercut · 37.5 s + slate

- **Logline:** New York's council subpoenaed SpaceXAI and threatened it with a judge. On 16 Oct the company
  testified, by video, through a government-affairs manager.
- **Insight:** the company that had to be subpoenaed came on a screen and gave no number for the odds of
  catastrophe. It did not know its catastrophe-insurance details, and it left owing the council a headcount
  of its safety staff.
- **Last line:** "Everybody is in charge of safety. / The headcount will follow." **…over** a hard hat
  passed hand to hand along the dais. It never lands on anyone, then settles on top of the monitor in
  the witness chair.
- **Beats (6 × 12 at 112 BPM, the City Hall chamber from `cw_*`):**
  1. The chamber, with the empty chair from Coxon #2. *"SpaceXAI was [subpoenaed]. / On 16 October, it came."*
     Tag: NEW YORK CITY HALL · 16.10.2026.
  2. A monitor is wheeled into the witness chair and its screen buffers. *"By [video]. / It sent a
     government-affairs manager."* A screen in the chair is staging, not reporting. Nothing in the
     coverage says what physically stood at the table.
  3. A binder marked CATASTROPHE slides across the table and bounces off the screen. *"Its catastrophe
     insurance? / 'Not aware of the [specifics]'."*
  4. A gavel taps once, and a scale weighs a coffin-shaped card against a rule book. *"If its AI causes a
     death? / 'We're responsible for following the [law].'"*
  5. The hard hat travels along the dais. *"How many people work on safety? / '[Everybody] is in charge of
     safety.'"*
  6. Coxon #2's odds dial is still blank. *"Odds of catastrophe: / [no number] given."* Then the last line.
- **Where / who:** the City Hall silhouette and the tag. The company is named because the story is unclear
  without it (README, "Naming in the films"). The witness is unnamed and shown with no face. The company's
  side is in the copy: it said safety is "core" and that it has "strict controls". If the cut allows,
  shot 2 can carry that as a second line.
- **Sources:** amNY, CNBC, Fox News live, NY Daily News, EFE, the Council's own release, all 16 Oct.
  Q2–Q4 in the pack each have 2+ outlets.
- **Risks:**
  - Say "no number was given". No outlet reports she was asked the 5 Oct question directly, so not
    "refused".
  - Leave out the Speaker's Grok image entirely, along with her "lying" remark.
  - No duration comparison: the hearing's length is unreported.
  - "Nobody gave a number" includes Anthropic.
- **Sound:** the Coxon palette (`SOUND.md`). A video-call chime replaces the gavel hit in shot 2, and the
  room goes silent before the last line.

## 2. Kill Switch · RFH · new look: the safety leaflet · 37.5 s + slate

- **Logline:** a US toaster's safety standard asks for a second off switch. California's AI kill switch is
  a study, due 16 November.
- **Insight:**
  - The toaster standard (UL 1026) asks for a second control in case the first fails.
  - US law requires a dangerous defect to be reported "immediately", which the regulator reads as within
    24 hours.
  - US federal rules for frontier AI are voluntary by their own text (EO 14409).
  - California's governor vetoed a required full-shutdown ability in 2024 (SB 1047). His 18 Sept order
    (N-9-26) asks experts to recommend one by 16 Nov.
- **Last line:** "The toaster has a second off switch. / It's in the leaflet." **…over** the AI leaflet
  folding shut on its empty warnings while the toaster beside it pops.
- **Beats (6 × 12):**
  1. The toaster's leaflet, full: eight pictograms. *"A toaster comes with a [leaflet]."* The number of
     warnings will be counted from a real toaster manual before it goes on screen.
  2. Its first switch jams, a second one clicks, and the smoke stops. *"Its US safety standard asks for a
     [second] off switch."*
  3. An envelope marked DEFECT drops into a postbox while a stopwatch runs. *"Find a dangerous defect? / Tell
     the regulator within [24 hours]."* Tag: CONSUMER PRODUCT SAFETY ACT § 15(b).
  4. The leaflet turns over to FRONTIER AI MODEL, with blank warnings. A sticker slaps on: VOLUNTARY.
     *"A frontier AI model. / US federal pre-release rules: [voluntary]."* Tag: WASHINGTON · EO 14409.
  5. A hand reaches for the big red button, which is a sticky note: STUDY · DUE 16 NOV. *"California's
     governor vetoed a [shutdown] rule in 2024. / In September he ordered a study."* Tag: SACRAMENTO ·
     ORDER N-9-26 · 18.9.2026.
  6. A washing-machine leaflet fills itself in. *Microsoft's president: "Right now, your washing machine is
     subject to more safety rules than AI."* Tag: BBC · 11.10.2026. Then the last line.
- **Sources:** UL 1026 revisions (InCompliance, Intertek), CPSC on §15(b), EO 14409 (CNBC, Crowell, CRS),
  N-9-26 (the governor's office, CalMatters, CNN, CNBC), SB 1047 veto (The Hill, TechRepublic), Smith
  (Engadget, Fox Business, The Next Web).
- **Risks:**
  - **"Toaster" is ours.** Smith said washing machine and milk, so the washing machine carries his name
    and the toaster never sits inside his quote marks.
  - "Fewer rules" holds only for **US federal, binding, pre-release** rules, so the copy says "US federal".
    California's SB 53 already requires incident reports, the EU AI Act's model duties are enforceable, and
    UL listing for a toaster is itself voluntary. The pack carries all of this.
  - The order is a study, not a mandate.
  - Microsoft sells AI. Name him as its president.
- **Art:** the leaflet look (`scenes/ks_kit.js`): an appliance leaflet on a dark worktop, one line weight,
  ISO pictograms, numbered callouts, and a black-and-yellow hazard tag kept above `RFP.SAFE_B`. Captions
  sit on the dark band above the leaflet. Its motion is the leaflet's own: the page turns, stickers slap
  on, switches click.
- **Sound:** a new palette, the sound of a kitchen: the toaster's tick, the pop, a sticker slap. The
  dryness is the joke.

## 3. Welcome Home · RFH · tech satire #2 (after Teslur) · clay · ~28 s, card, no slate

- **Logline:** the launch film of a home display that knows your face, remade in clay. It ends the way
  the real one does: the family uses the device's camera to watch their daughter leave.
- **Insight:**
  - The spoken promise, that your data is not kept on the device once you leave, covers the personal data
    it borrows from your phone.
  - No storage or retention terms have been published for the faces it recognises, or for a visitor's face.
  - The real launch film ends with the family watching the daughter leave on the home camera.
- **Last line (closing card, in the Teslur style):** **WELCOME HOME** in clay letters, then a white
  question with a thump: "Your guests?" It is a question because the terms are unpublished, not shown to
  be bad.
- **Beats (5):**
  1. A clay launch shot: the device on the island, morning light, the cord dropping through a hole drilled
     in the island (the real film did this).
  2. A family of masked Tokos in the morning chaos. The device's face box finds each mask: "Welcome home."
  3. The daughter slips out of the door. The device's dome swivels after her, owl-like.
  4. The whole family crowds round the 7-inch screen to watch the door camera: her and a boyfriend.
  5. The boyfriend, in a grey mask, looks into the lens. On screen: "GUEST: ?" Cut to the card.
- **Optional Helsinki tag:** it is not sold in the EU. A last beat could have the device in a Kallio
  kitchen showing "Not available in your region".
- **Sources:** Apple Newsroom and CNBC (13 Oct) for the product and the six faces; Reuters and bluewin for
  "personal data stays on the iPhone"; ABC for the "once you leave" line (single-source, paraphrase only:
  not quoted on screen); The Verge, via Digital Today and Analytics Insight, plus Inc. and Creative Bloq for
  the film's ending; Macworld for the missing hardware camera switch (one outlet).
- **Risks:**
  - The brand is renamed (as with Doodle and Teslur), with no logo, no assistant's name and no Snoopy face.
  - The film's look is copied, never its footage or music.
  - No real faces: Tokos in masks only.
  - Never imply the faces are kept; the terms are unstated.
- **Art:** the look test is `clay-home/film-wh.js`, built on the Teslur clay kit: a high-key kitchen, a
  sage island, a rose-pink device on its domed speaker, and Toko in the magenta original. **Needed before
  building:** frames from the real launch film ("Welcome Apple HomeView: Designed for life at home",
  YouTube). The research could not watch it, and Teslur only became exact once the owner sent broadcast
  frames.
- **Sound:** a bright launch-film piano bed, a face-ID "ding" per family member, a doorbell-camera chirp,
  the room going quiet, then the thump on the card.
