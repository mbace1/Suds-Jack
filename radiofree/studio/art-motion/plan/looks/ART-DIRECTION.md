# Art direction: Kill Switch and Welcome Home (17 Oct 2026)

Owner: "act as an art director and think of refining these further in their own styles." This is what
the v1 look tests got wrong, what each look should become, and how far the tools can take it.

## Kill Switch: the leaflet

**What v1 is:** a clean vector infographic. It reads at a glance, but it is a drawing *of* a leaflet.
Nothing about it says paper, ink or kitchen, and the joke needs all three: this is the boring object
that ships in the box with every toaster.

**Target: a macro photograph of a real leaflet on a real worktop.** The camera is a person leaning
over the counter at 11 at night, reading the small print.

| | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Object | a flat sheet, a drop shadow | **3D paper**: accordion-folded, not quite flat, the creases catching light, the sheet's own soft shadow on the stone |
| Print | perfect vector colour | **real spot-colour printing**: black, safety yellow, red and blue plates, each a hair off register; greys as a 45° halftone screen; slightly uneven ink; the black overprinting |
| Type | grey bars standing in for text | **real text in ten languages, Finnish first**: "Älä upota laitetta veteen." Real manual furniture: IMPORTANT SAFEGUARDS, SAVE THESE INSTRUCTIONS, page numbers, the rating plate (230 V~ 50 Hz 850 W) |
| Figure | none | **the instruction man**, the manual's little line-drawn person, wearing a Toko mask: he unplugs the toaster, keeps it away from water |
| Set | dark flat laminate | **dark honed granite** with flecks, **crumbs**, the **chrome corner of the toaster** soft at the frame edge, its cable |
| Light | even | **one warm pendant pool** on the leaflet, falling off to near black at the top of frame, which is where the captions sit |
| Lens | none | **macro**: shallow depth of field, so the far edge of the sheet goes soft |
| The kill switch | a drawn button with a tag | **a sticky note**: a red button drawn on a Post-it in marker, "STUDY, DUE 16 NOV" in handwriting, stuck to the AI leaflet and lifting at one corner |

The AI model's leaflet is the **back of the same sheet**: same layout, same languages, but every line is
empty. The flip between them is the film's one big camera move.

## Welcome Home: the clay launch film

**What v1 is:** the right palette and a charming device, in a room nobody lives in. Haze and flat light
make it read as a CG render, not as a set someone built and lit.

**Target: a launch film shot on a stop-motion stage.** It should have the airy, expensive morning light
of the real thing, and every surface should be visibly made by hand.

| | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Light | flat, hazy | **low morning sun through the window**: hard-edged warm patches across the island and the floor, light shafts with dust, a cool fill from the room, real contact shadows. Bright, but with a shadow side |
| Set | bare walls | a **lived-in kitchen**: glazed clay zellige backsplash (every tile a little different), white-oak cabinets with brass pulls, two **pendant lamps** over the island, open shelves with jars, plants, a kettle |
| The real film's details | none | **the cord dropping through a hole drilled in the island**, and French toast in a pan |
| Device | a home screen | **the Flow face**: a soft glowing colour field that leans toward whoever is nearest; its light spills onto the counter |
| Cast | one Toko | **a family of masked Tokos**, one carrier colour each: the device's whole idea is telling faces apart, and these faces are masks |
| Device POV | a clean render plus a box | **a camera feed**: wide lens, a hint of barrel distortion, auto-exposure, the face box springing in, the greeting on a **frosted-glass** pill that blurs the room behind it |
| New look 3 | none | **the climax**: the family crowded round the 7-inch screen watching the door camera, faces lit by the screen |

## What stays

- Captions and the station bug stay in the house style.
- Tags stay above `RFP.SAFE_B`.
- No real faces, no brand names or logos.
- Everything is drawn or modelled in code, with no stock images.
