# Pre-production review: films 01–07 (15 Oct 2026)

**Reviewer:** an independent editor agent that has not seen any build. **Disclosure:** this
reviewer is also Claude, an Anthropic model, and Anthropic appears in films 1 and 2. The
owner should check the balance in those two.

**Method:**
- I read PLANNING.md, the slate, the seven shot tables and the three research packs.
- I read only the captions, in order, as a viewer with no background would.
- I spot-checked every key claim with standard web search. Page fetches to inquirer.com,
  aiweekly.co and resultsense.com failed in this sandbox, so a claim marked *unconfirmed*
  could not be read in its original.
- I counted the characters of every caption with a script.

**The reading budget I used:**
- **Papercut shot (6.4 s):** line one at 0.8 s and line two at 3.0 s, both gone about
  0.35 s before the dissolve (≈ 5.55 s).
  - A first caption followed by a second gets 2.2 s, so it can be at most **~24 characters**.
  - A second caption can be at most **~31 characters**.
  - A single caption gets 4.75 s, so it can be at most **~75 characters**.
  - In shot 6 (5.4 s, no dissolve), the second caption can be at most **~28 characters**.
- **Clay beat:** the captions plus a 0.3 s lead must fit the beat.

**Verdicts**

The next most common is a picture that says something the caption does not.

## 1 · Paid For By: GO WITH FIXES

**Clarity.** The argument is clear: AI money pays for ads that do not mention AI.

Three problems:
- **The two groups read as one bloc.** The insight is "both sides of the AI industry", but
  nothing on screen says the two groups are rivals. Leading the Future wants fewer rules and
  Public First wants guardrails (NBC, CNN).
- **Shot 4 does two jobs.** It covers AI and data centres.
- **Shot 3 lists topics that are not in the sources.** The topics are jobs, prices, crime,
  family, farms and flags.

**The joke.** It is good and it is set up, because the fine print in shot 1 is paid off in
shot 6. At 89 characters across 5.4 s, though, nobody can read it.
- Cut it to: `Paid for by AI,` / `which asked not · to be [mentioned].`
- That keeps the disclaimer's own form, and the shrinking fine print answers it.

**Pictures.**
- **Shot 1:** the fine print has to read as a strip that says PAID FOR BY. It can be tiny
  but it must be visible, or shot 6 has no setup.
- **Shot 3:** six word-cards at once is too many at phone size. Flash three or four, one at
  a time, using the caption's own words. Use PRICES, not GAS.
- **Shot 4:** cut the DATA CENTRES card, because a card cannot "search".
- **Shot 5:** a keyboard typing **by itself** suggests AI-written ads. That is a different
  story (the Wesleyan count of AI-generated ads).
  - Use a magenta-gloved hand, the money, to do the typing.
  - A and I sit far apart on a QWERTY keyboard, so crop tight and put a cash band on each.

**Facts.**

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| $55.7M, 95 unique ads, NYT review | **Holds.** Confirmed by AI Weekly, The Outpost and the Inquirer (per the pack). |
| "Aligned with" | **Use this wording.** It is the phrasing in every write-up. Use it rather than "tied to". OpenAI reportedly distanced itself from Leading the Future in June (ValueAdd VC, single source). |
| "Only 7 of 95" | **Still only one secondary source** (AI Weekly). Other copy says "only some". Confirm it from the NYT or Inquirer text before rendering. Fallback: `Few of the 95 · mention AI.` |
| "None mention data centres" | **Cut it.** It is true only of the 95 ads reviewed. Build American AI, Leading the Future's issue-ad arm, is reported to be running pro-data-centre ads in KS, OH and WI (TechCrunch 31 Aug on the plan; ValueAdd VC). |
| Election day 3 Nov | **Holds.** |
| Shot 3 topics | **Unsourced.** Source them from the NYT text, or use NBC's finding instead: `They're about everything · [except AI].` |

**Reading time.**
- Shot 1 runs to 86 characters and shot 6 to 89; both are over.
- Shot 4's first caption is 28 characters; it is over if a second caption follows.

**Fixes (captions).**
1. Single caption: `Rival groups aligned · with OpenAI and Anthropic · spent [$55.7 million].` (66 characters). The tag US MIDTERMS · NOV 3 carries the election.
4. Single caption: `Only [7] of the 95 · mention AI.`
   - Change the tag to NYT ANALYSIS · 30.9.2026.
   - Drop data centres from the caption and the picture.
6. `Paid for by AI,` / `which asked not · to be [mentioned].`

## 2 · On the Same Voluntary Terms: GO WITH FIXES

**Clarity.** Two jumps would lose a viewer:
- **Shot 3:** "asked labs to hold back" leaves out what was held back and from whom.
- **Shot 2:** "the companies" has not been introduced.

**Place.** A green door is not London. Add a pillar box and the Elizabeth Tower in
silhouette. In shot 3, put the White House in silhouette on the far shore.

**The joke.** "The tests are voluntary. / So, it turns out, is the safety." is an editorial
claim about safety in general. It also ignores the film's best setup, the red phone. A
callback is sharper and fair:
- `Testing has resumed.` / `Until the phone · [rings again].`
- This is the station's line. Commentators note that a future US request could restore the
  hold.
- **Picture:** the OPEN · BY INVITATION card is hung, then the red phone on the far shore
  gives one small rattle, and the door clicks shut.

**Pictures.**
- **Shot 3 is misleading.** The White House asked the **labs**, not the Institute. The red
  phone must ring at the labs' end (the US shore), and the strings are pulled from there.
- **Reel away two boxes of three.** The request named OpenAI and Anthropic. Google DeepMind
  was not named in it, though accounts differ. Restoration covered all three.
- **Shot 4:** end the calendar on a large "17".

**Facts.**

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| 24 Sep to 11 Oct, 17 days, from the Office of the National Cyber Director | **Holds.** Several outlets. |
| Access restored for OpenAI, Anthropic and DeepMind on the voluntary basis | **Holds.** |
| The minister's exact words "same voluntary terms" | **Not confirmed.** They appear in no excerpt I could reach, so **drop the quote marks** unless the Reuters text is in hand. Paraphrase never goes in quotes. |
| "The testers waited 17 days" | **Overclaims.** The Institute's director disputed a blanket freeze (per the pack), and one French source dates an AISI GPT-6 Astra report to 28 Sep, inside the window. Say the hold lasted 17 days. |
| The UK is open to regulation if the voluntary system falls short (minister to Reuters, 3 Aug) | **Context only**, not for the screen. It supports the joke. |

**Reading time.**
- Shot 1 is 76 characters and shot 2 is 66; both are over.
- Shot 6 is 0.2 s over.
- Lines of 29–31 characters appear in shots 1, 3 and 5.

**Fixes (captions).**
1. Single caption: `Britain's AI Security · Institute tests new AI · [before] it is released.`
2. `Only when [invited].` / `No law makes · the labs say yes.`
3. Single caption: `The White House asked · two US labs to [hold back] · new models from the UK.` Tag: THE WHITE HOUSE · WASHINGTON · 24.9.2026.
4. `The hold lasted` / `[17 days].`
5. `Then access came back,` / `on the same · [voluntary] terms.` No quote marks. Tag: TELEGRAPH · REUTERS · 11.10.2026.
6. `Testing has resumed.` / `Until the phone · [rings again].`

## 3 · The Newsreader Was Fake: GO WITH FIXES

**Clarity.** The film is strong, but two captions trip a viewer:
- **Shot 2:** "asked Meta to stop them" could mean stop the newsreaders.
- **Shot 4:** "Meta's papers charged" says the papers did the charging.
- **The court beat never says why the auction matters.** The court's reason (Meta decides
  who sees which ad, so it is not a passive host) *is* the insight. Put it in the caption.

**The joke.** "The newsreader was fake. / The invoice was real." lands. It is set up by the
fake ad in shot 1 and the price in shot 4. **Keep it.** Make the register's paper read
INVOICE.

**Pictures.**
- **Shot 2 contradicts itself.** With the newsreaders' backs to us, their cards face the
  studio camera, and we see only the blank backs. Draw them front-on as dark card
  silhouettes with no faces, as *A Handful of People* did.
- **Shot 1:** no Yle or MTV logos on the scam ad. Imitating them is the scam. Use a generic
  NEWS desk.
- **Shot 5:** **German judges do not use gavels.** Use a robed judge, a judgment headed
  URTEIL, and the LIABLE stamp. Put the Frankfurt skyline in the window so the city reads.
- **Shot 4:** the pinned card should read META: A DETERRENT at a size you can read, because
  it carries Meta's side.

**Facts.**

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Yle and MTV campaign from 14 April, asking Meta to remove fakes | **Holds.** Alma Media, MTV. |
| Frankfurt Regional Court, 16.9.2026: Meta liable, hosting defence rejected because its algorithms decide who sees what and in what order | **Holds.** TNW, Silicon, MLex, Notebookcheck. |
| The ruling can be appealed | **Holds.** Meta: "respectfully disagree". |
| Reuters, Nov 2025: penalty bids for suspected fraudsters, below a 95% certainty ban threshold | **Holds.** Meta calls the estimates rough and over-inclusive. Meta says the higher rates make suspicious advertisers less competitive, i.e. a deterrent. |
| Overall | **Fair as written.** |

**Reading time.**
- Shot 4 is 102 characters and shot 5 is 75 across two captions; both are far over.
- Two lines run to 27–28 characters.

**Fixes (captions).**
2. Single caption: `In April, they asked Meta · to [remove] the fakes.`
4. Single caption: `Reuters: in Meta's papers, · suspected scammers · were charged [more].`

5. Single caption: `A German court: Meta picks · who sees each ad, · so it is [liable].`
   - Tag: FRANKFURT REGIONAL COURT · 16.9.2026 · CAN BE APPEALED.
6. Keep.

## 4 · Irreversible: GO WITH FIXES

**Clarity and fairness.** These are the same problem here.
- **What Doodle actually argues:** that the Commission's method does **not** anonymise
  enough.
- **Shot 2 drops that.** So shot 3 ("the law already says irreversibly anonymised") reads as
  a gotcha implying Doodle's case is empty. The research pack itself warns against that.
- **The fix:** state both sides plainly in shot 5. The real point (shots 4 and 6: the
  company warning you is the one that keeps your history) does not need the gotcha to be
  unfair.

**The joke.** It lands and is set up by the cabinet in shot 4. It is crisper as
`Your searches: [protected].` / `From everyone · but Doodle.` The original is 66 characters
in a 5.4 s shot, which is over.

**Pictures.**
- **Shots 5 and 6 both use a padlock, with opposite meanings.** In shot 5 it stands for the
  EU's safeguards; in shot 6 it is Doodle's lock. Keep the padlock for the ending. In
  shot 5, use two speech cards, one in Doodle's colours and one in EU blue, over a scale.
- **Establish the paper tape early.** In shot 1 or shot 4, show searches spooling from the
  bar into the cabinet. Otherwise "copying the tape" in shot 6 is new information at the
  punchline.
- **Give the hand a four-colour cuff** so it reads as Doodle's.
- **Shot 1:** the envelope text is too long. Use SHARE DATA plus the stars.
- **Shot 2:** draw the Court of Justice's twin gold towers so Luxembourg reads.

**Facts.**

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Euronews 29 Sep: "irreversible harm" | **Holds.** |
| The July orders, sharing due Jan 2027 | **Holds.** |
| Interim measures requested | **Holds.** No ruling found as of today; re-check before posting. |
| Recital 61's "irreversibly altered" | **Holds**, as cited by the blogs. |
| Commission: "robust safeguards" | **Holds.** |

**Reading time.** Shots 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 are all over as two-caption shots.

**Fixes (captions).**
1. Single caption: `The EU ordered Doodle · to share [anonymised] · search data with rivals.`
2. Single caption: `Doodle went to court, · warning of "[irreversible] · harm" to privacy.`
3. Single caption: `The law already says · the data must be · [irreversibly] anonymised.`
4. Single caption: `The search history · it is protecting · is the one [it keeps].`
5. Single caption: `Doodle: not anonymous enough. · The EU: it has [safeguards].`
6. `Your searches: [protected].` / `From everyone · but Doodle.`

## 7 · Somebody Does the Dishes: GO WITH FIXES

**Clarity.** "Because a person was steering it" makes a causal claim the review does not support.
Stern found the robot slow and clumsy *even while* remote-controlled.

**Inconsistency.** The header's "joke at the end" is the dishwasher wobble, but in the table
that is beat 4, not the ending.

**The joke.** "Now he's just further away" lands, and the beat-1 price is its stake. The
"watching" half of the insight has no payoff yet. **Picture fix:** Toko waves at the
robot's camera eye, and in the cubicle the man waves back. That is a wordless second laugh
and it is fair: in 1X's Expert Mode, operators see through the cameras with the owner's
approval.

**Pictures.**
- **Beat 3:** pulling back *through the kitchen wall* says the operator is next door. Where
  the WSJ operator sat is not documented. Keep the pull-back and give the cubicle a night
  window against the sunny kitchen, so "somewhere else" reads at no cost.
- **Beat 2:** a wall clock jumping five minutes sells "slowly". The WSJ took more than five
  minutes for a few dishes.

**Facts.**

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| WSJ hands-on, 28 Oct 2025: teleoperated with a VR headset, slow, nearly toppled | **Holds.** Daring Fireball, Decrypt, Road to VR. |
| $20,000 or $499 a month | **Holds.** |
| The test is now a year old | **Date it on screen.** Deliveries were due in 2026, with a factory opened in April. One July analysis found no confirmed customer delivery. |

**Reading time.**
- Beat 3 is 49 characters in 4.4 s and beat 5 is 58 in 4.2 s; both are over.
- Beat 4 has a 28-character line.
- The beats add up to 20.6 s, as in film 6.

**Fixes.**
- Beat 2: `In a reviewer's 2025 test: · [slowly].`
- Beat 3: single caption, `A person in a VR headset · was [steering it].`
- Beat 4: `Its maker says · it will learn · to [work alone].`
- Beat 5: `Somebody always · did the dishes.` / `Now he's · [further away].`
