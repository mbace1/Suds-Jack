# Review: "A Handful of People" v2 (hp_v2_captions.mp4)

**Score: 6.5/10.** **Verdict: not ready.** The numbers, dates and post text check out. There are two fairness problems and one picture that contradicts its caption. Fix findings 1–3 and this can ship.

## What checks out

- **The post.** "I resigned from Anthropic today." is the opening line and "Neither company is acting responsibly." is verbatim (Fortune, NPR/WVIA, TNW). Outlets disagree on 8 vs 9 Sept, so "September 2026" is the safe label.
- **Reuters/Ipsos, 7 Oct.** 62% of registered voters say AI "could get out of control and risk the future of humankind", and 54% say Congress has not taken AI risks "seriously enough or at all". "Isn't taking AI risks seriously enough" is a fair condensation: "not at all" falls inside "not enough".
- **The election date.** 3 Nov 2026 is a Tuesday and is the midterm date.
- **"Campaign issue".** Defensible: Pew (8 Oct) has AI as a "very important" vote factor for 44% of voters. One counterweight: NYT/Siena found under 1% name it as their *top* issue.
- **The Speaker.** "Should not be rushed" is a fair paraphrase. Axios has him saying action "must be done carefully". The word itself is on record from CNN the same day: "If Congress rushes to hold emergency sessions…". Add CNN to the source note.
- **No faces.** Coxon and Stewart are faceless silhouettes, Coxon's avatar is a blank disc, and the Speaker is a podium.
- **Loudness.** −13.6 LUFS integrated against the house −14, true peak −1.9 dBFS. The final brass note rings into the fade from 36.6 to 37.4 rather than being chopped.
- **Captions.** The widest line is about 640 px, or about 750 px while the highlight pops. None is clipped. Captions sit at y 270–590, clear of every key object.

## Findings

1. **HIGH, 7.2–12.5: "he and Jon Stewart agreed" is not on record.** The only text I found carrying the phrase is a Substack transcription with Coxon saying it "can't be the decision of a handful of… people". Parade has Stewart *asking* who gets to decide. Neither shows Stewart agreeing, so the line puts a position in Stewart's mouth.
   - **Fix:** "On The Daily Show, he told Jon Stewart / the choice shouldn't rest / with [a handful of people]." This is still a paraphrase with no quote marks, and it is Coxon's own point.
2. **HIGH, 25.7–32.1: the edit sets Coxon against a position close to his own.** On Meet the Press, Coxon said Congress should let the labs police themselves while it drafts a flexible long-term framework, and that current bills would soon be outpaced. Johnson's "don't rush; meet industry leaders first" is close to that interim stance. Cutting from Coxon, to the hands, to a mocked "rushed" makes him look like he wants a fast law and opposes interim self-regulation.
   - **Fix:** aim the beat at where they actually differ. Axios, 13 Sept: Johnson "says Congress won't take the lead". Use "The Speaker of the House / says Congress won't / [take the lead]." Coxon wants lawmakers drafting a framework, so this contrast is fair to him.
3. **HIGH, 26.5–32.1: the hourglass contradicts its caption.** After the flip the top bulb is empty and the bottom is still full (frames 26.4, 26.8, 27.3, 31.5). That reads as "time's up", not "slow". A notch also appears in the sand pile beside the snail at about 31.5.
   - **Fix:** after the turn, put the sand in the top bulb and run a hairline stream. The top should fall only about 10 px and the bottom should grow to match over five seconds. Mask the snail off the glass.
4. **MEDIUM, 0–6.4: "Neither company" has no antecedent.** The ellipsis drops the sentence that names OpenAI, so viewers are left asking "neither of which?". The bottom third of the card is empty.
   - **Fix:** restore "I spent the last three years doing pretraining research at both OpenAI and Anthropic." at a slightly smaller size. Alternatively, keep the ellipsis and show "…at both OpenAI and Anthropic."
5. **MEDIUM, 7.0–12.9: the hand reads as theirs.** It rises from between Coxon and Stewart at head height, under a caption about "a handful of people".
   - **Fix:** bring the cuffed hand down from the top of frame, or set it clearly behind the backdrop.
6. **MEDIUM: dead stretches.** About 20 of the 37.5 s are near-still holds:
   - 3.6–6.4: the post sits finished.
   - **7.0–12.9: nothing happens except a globe bob.** This is the weakest stretch.
   - 15.0–19.3: the hands hold after the stamp.
   - 22.0–25.7: the Capitol holds after the windows go dark.
   - 27.2–32.1: the hourglass holds after the flip.

   **Fix:** give the desk shot an event, for example the hand tightening its grip or the globe being lifted out of reach, and start each later event about 1 s later.
7. **LOW-MEDIUM, 16.6–19.1 and 24.3–26.6: the bed drops to digital silence.** There are pockets of −inf between pizz hits while the crowd of hands is still on screen, which makes the score feel like it stops (LRA 10.4).
   - **Fix:** keep a low crowd or room tone under shots 3–5.
8. **LOW, 32.4–35.1: the ballots come from nowhere.** The "many hands" are two dark, cropped shapes in the bottom corners, and the ballots fly in from mid-air. The "lid snap" on the 35.14 hit is a twitch of about 4 px.
   - **Fix:** bring the hands into frame above the slot. Start the magenta ring exactly on the hit, since it currently starts about 0.1 s late.
9. **LOW, phone legibility.**
   - The snail reads as a spiral bun.
   - "September 2026" on the card is about 28 px tall.
   - The Capitol windows going dark are small and partly cut by the frame.
   - At 6.2 the card's lower-right corner tucks under the bezel.

   **Fix:** enlarge the snail and give it a head, or cut it. Enlarge the date text. Stop the push-in about 10 px earlier.
10. **LOW, paperwork.** The shot table no longer matches the film:
    - It has "a researcher" where the film names Jacob Coxon.
    - It describes a 54% banner the film does not have.
    - It describes a lid snap that is not visible.
    - It writes "3 November" where the film says "November 3".

    Also add Axios, CNN and the NBC article on Coxon's self-regulation remark to the sources, and note the 5 vs 6 Oct episode-date conflict (Rolling Stone / Mary Sue). The date is not on screen.
